Skin care for teachers in India needs to handle four everyday things: chalk or marker dust that dries out hands and face, sun at morning assembly and playground duty, long hours of talking with little water, and late nights correcting papers. A gentle face wash, sunscreen before school, a hand cream in your bag, lip balm and a simple night cream cover most of it in under five minutes a day.

Most school days start early and end late, so a long routine is not realistic. Below is what a school day does to skin, a short routine that fits around it, and what to keep in your staffroom drawer.

Before school: a 3-minute morning routine

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash and lukewarm water.
  2. Apply a light moisturiser, a pea-sized amount for the whole face. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has avocado and aloe vera only, with no kojic acid, so it works well in the day.
  3. Apply sunscreen, broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, to the face, neck and the backs of your hands. Do this even if you will mostly be indoors; assembly, duty and the commute add up.
  4. Lip balm as the last step, and keep the same one in your bag.

If you wear makeup to school, keep it light. A full base under chalk dust and sweat for eight hours is hard on pores.

Chalk dust and dry hands

Chalk is mostly a dry mineral powder, and it soaks up moisture from your fingers. Add frequent hand washing and duster dust, and many teachers end up with rough, cracked fingertips, especially in winter.

  • Tap chalk dust off your hands before washing, then wash with a mild soap and lukewarm water.
  • Apply hand cream every time you wash, while your hands are slightly damp. A small tube fits in a pencil pouch.
  • At night, apply a thicker layer of cream or petroleum jelly on cracked fingertips and wear cotton socks or gloves if you can.
  • Try not to touch or rub your face and eyes while teaching. If dust gets into your eyes, rinse them with clean water and do not rub.
  • If you can choose, dustless chalk or a chalk holder reduces the amount on your skin.

Sun on duty days

Morning assembly, PT periods, annual-day practice and sports day are where most teachers pick up a tan without noticing. On ground duty, stand in the shade where you can, carry an umbrella or wear a cap, and reapply sunscreen at lunch if you will be outside again in the afternoon. The night routine on busy nights

Correction weeks and exam duty are when routines fall apart. Keep it to three steps you can do even when tired:

  1. Cleanse to wash off sunscreen, chalk dust and sweat. Cleanse twice if you wore makeup.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping sunscreen because you work indoors. Duty, assembly and the commute are enough sun to tan over a term.
  • Washing your face many times with soap. It leaves skin tighter. Rinse with water at lunch and use a face wash only morning and night.
  • Licking dry lips while talking. It makes them drier. Use lip balm instead.
  • Starting several new products before a school event. Try one new product at a time, and patch test first; our patch test guide shows how.

When to see a doctor

See a dermatologist or doctor if your hands crack and bleed, itch badly or develop a rash that does not settle with moisturising, if your eyes stay red or watery after chalk dust, or if you have breathing trouble in dusty classrooms. These need proper medical advice, not just a cream.

Short answers

Is chalk dust bad for skin? Chalk dust mostly dries skin, especially on the fingers. Washing it off gently and using hand cream after usually keeps hands comfortable.

How can teachers stop dry, cracked hands? Tap off chalk, wash with mild soap and lukewarm water, apply hand cream every time, and use a thick layer at night on cracked fingertips.

Do teachers need sunscreen if they teach indoors? Yes, if you do assembly, ground duty or commute in daylight. Apply SPF 30+ before school and again before outdoor duty.

What is a quick skincare routine for teachers? Face wash, light moisturiser and sunscreen in the morning; cleanse, night cream, hand cream and lip balm at night.

Why are my lips always dry after teaching? Talking for hours and not drinking enough water dry them out. Keep lip balm and a water bottle on your table.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
