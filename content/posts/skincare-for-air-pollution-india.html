Skincare for air pollution in India comes down to three habits: wash the day's dust and smoke off gently every night, put a comfortable moisturiser back on, and wear sunscreen every morning. You do not need an "anti-pollution" label; you need a routine you can repeat through the smoggy months from late October to February.

When the AQI climbs in Delhi, the NCR and many North Indian cities, skin often feels dull, dry, gritty or more easily irritated by evening. This guide explains what pollution does to the surface of your skin, gives a simple day and night plan, and lists the mistakes that make things worse.

What air pollution does to your skin

Smog is a mix of fine dust, smoke, vehicle exhaust and gases. Your skin is an outer barrier, so much of this simply settles on it and mixes with sweat, oil and sunscreen through the day.

  • A dull, grey film. Fine particles sit on the skin and in the pores, so the face can look tired even after a good night's sleep.
  • Dryness and tightness. Winter smog usually comes with cold, dry air and AC or heaters indoors, which pull water out of the skin.
  • More sensitivity. Many people notice stinging, redness or itching on bad-air days, especially if the skin barrier is already stressed.
  • Oxidative stress. Pollution and UV both create free radicals on the skin's surface. This is why antioxidants and sunscreen come up so often in pollution advice.

None of this is fixed by one product. What helps is removing the build-up kindly and keeping the barrier comfortable. For the barrier side, see night cream for skin barrier.

Pollution skincare routine: day and night

Morning

  1. Rinse or a light wash. Dry skin can use plain lukewarm water; oily skin can use a mild face wash.
  2. Light moisturiser. A thin layer gives the skin a buffer before you step out.
  3. Sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher. Smog can make the sky look hazy, but UV still gets through. Use about two finger-lengths for face and neck.
  4. Cover up when you can. A scarf, dupatta or mask over the lower face cuts down how much dust lands on your skin on a commute.

Night

  1. Take off sunscreen and grime first with micellar water on cotton or a little cleansing oil or balm, so you do not need to scrub.
  2. Wash with a gentle face wash and lukewarm water for 30 to 60 seconds. Use your fingertips, not a rough towel or scrubber.
  3. Pat dry and leave the skin a little damp.
  4. Night cream. A pea-sized amount for the face and a little more for the neck. See how to apply night cream for the method.
  5. Lips and hands too. A balm on lips and a cream on hands helps on dry, smoky days.

Ingredients that fit a pollution routine

Mistakes that make pollution skin worse

  • Scrubbing every night. Grainy scrubs and rough cloths damage the barrier and leave skin more reactive. Gentle exfoliation once a week is plenty.
  • Washing four or five times a day. Twice is enough. More washing strips oil and leaves skin tight.
  • Very hot water. It feels good on a cold evening but dries the face fast.
  • Sleeping with sunscreen or makeup on. Grime sits on the skin all night. Cleanse first, always.
  • Skipping sunscreen because the sky is grey. Haze is not protection.
  • Starting several new actives in smog season. Add one new product at a time; see how to introduce active ingredients.

Commuters, bike riders and outdoor workers

If you spend an hour or more in traffic, carry a small pouch: a pack of plain wet wipes or a travel bottle of micellar water for a quick wipe on arrival, a lip balm and your sunscreen to top up at lunch. On a two-wheeler, cover your face with a clean cotton scarf and wash it often. When to see a doctor

Creams help skin feel comfortable; they do not fix a medical problem. See a dermatologist if you get a rash, burning, swelling, eczema that flares every winter, or acne that turns painful. If pollution also brings breathing trouble, cough or eye irritation, please see a doctor and follow local health advisories.

Short answers

What is the best skincare routine for pollution? Sunscreen and a light moisturiser in the morning; at night, remove sunscreen, wash gently, and apply a night cream. Repeat daily through the smog months.

Do I need an anti-pollution cream? Not necessarily. Gentle cleansing, a comfortable moisturiser and daily sunscreen matter more than the label on the jar.

Should I double cleanse in Delhi pollution? At night, yes, if you wear sunscreen or makeup: a cleansing balm or micellar water first, then a mild face wash. Keep both gentle.

Can pollution make skin dull? Yes, dust and dryness can make skin look dull and tired. Cleansing and moisturising usually bring back a fresher look within days.

Is steam good for removing pollution from skin? Frequent hot steam can dry and irritate skin. A gentle wash with lukewarm water is enough.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
