Each one works in a slightly different way to slow extra pigment, so dark spots and acne marks look less noticeable over 8 to 12 weeks when you also wear sunscreen every day.

How dark spot ingredients work

Dark spots are areas where skin made extra melanin, often after sun, heat or a pimple. Spot-focused ingredients usually do one of three things:

  • Slow pigment making. Kojic acid, alpha arbutin and licorice extract calm tyrosinase, the enzyme skin uses to make melanin.
  • Slow pigment transfer. Niacinamide reduces how much pigment moves up into the visible skin cells.
  • Protect and calm. Vitamin C and azelaic acid help with the oxidative stress and inflammation that can trigger new marks.

None of them change your natural skin colour. They work on extra pigment, and the existing spot looks less noticeable as skin renews, roughly every 4 to 6 weeks.

Niacinamide for uneven skin tone

Niacinamide (vitamin B3) is one of the easiest ingredients to live with. It helps tone look more even, supports the skin barrier, and helps oily skin look less shiny. It pairs comfortably with most other ingredients, including kojic acid and alpha arbutin. If your skin is oily, see night cream for oily skin for light textures that suit it.

Alpha arbutin for dark spots

Alpha arbutin is a stable form of arbutin, found in plants such as bearberry. It is water-soluble, light and usually gentle, which makes it a good first choice for sensitive skin. It works slowly, so give it the full 8 to 12 weeks.

Kojic acid skincare benefits

Kojic acid is made by fungi during fermentation, for example of rice. It has a long history in Indian and Asian creams for dark spots and tanning. Benefits: steady results on spots and tan marks, and it pairs well with niacinamide. Downsides: it can tingle or irritate sensitive skin and can darken in heat and light, so store it well. Our kojic acid cream for dark spots guide covers how to use it.

Azelaic acid skincare benefits and azelaic acid for acne marks

Azelaic acid is naturally found in grains such as barley and wheat. It is popular because it does two jobs at once: it helps calm breakout-prone skin and helps acne marks and redness look less noticeable. That makes it a common choice for people who still get pimples and have brown marks. Lower strengths appear in cosmetic creams; higher strengths are usually prescribed by dermatologists. Expect some tingling in the first weeks. Please note: no Mirc Glow cream contains azelaic acid.

Licorice extract for skin care

Licorice root extract contains glabridin, which helps calm the look of redness and slow extra pigment. It is often found in gentle "calming" creams and serums and suits sensitive skin. It is usually a supporting ingredient rather than the star. No Mirc Glow cream contains licorice.

How to choose and combine

  1. Start with one spot-focused product plus sunscreen. Add more only after 4 weeks of calm skin.
  2. Sensitive skin: niacinamide, alpha arbutin or licorice first.
  3. Breakouts plus marks: niacinamide, or azelaic acid with your dermatologist's guidance.
  4. Tan and spots on normal skin: kojic acid with niacinamide is a classic pair.
  5. Always patch test for 24 to 48 hours first.

Short answers

What ingredients help with dark spots the most? Niacinamide, alpha arbutin, kojic acid, azelaic acid, licorice extract and vitamin C are the best-known options, always with daily sunscreen.

Which ingredient is gentlest for sensitive skin? Niacinamide, alpha arbutin and licorice extract are usually the gentlest.

Is azelaic acid good for acne marks? Yes, it is popular for breakout-prone skin with marks. Higher strengths are usually prescribed.

Can I use niacinamide and vitamin C together? Yes. Most people can use both, in one routine or at different times of day.

How long do these ingredients take? Usually 8 to 12 weeks of regular use with sunscreen for a more even look.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
