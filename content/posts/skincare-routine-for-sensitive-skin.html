The best skincare routine for sensitive skin is short and gentle: a mild, fragrance-free cleanser, a soothing moisturiser and a sunscreen in the morning, and just cleanser plus moisturiser at night. Fewer products means fewer chances of stinging, redness or bumps, and it is easier to spot what your skin does not like.

Sensitive skin in India deals with a lot: strong sun, sweat, pollution, hard water and sudden switches from heat to AC. Here is a full day and night plan, how to add new products safely, and where soothing ingredients like panthenol fit in.

What "sensitive skin" usually means

Sensitive skin is not a single skin type. It describes skin that reacts easily. You might have it if:

  • Products often sting, burn or itch for more than a few seconds.
  • Your face goes red quickly with heat, spicy food, sun or rubbing.
  • Fragranced products or new face washes give you small bumps or rough patches.
  • Your skin feels tight and uncomfortable even when it is not very dry.

Sensitive skin can also be oily, dry or combination. Once you know your base type (our skin quiz can help), choose textures for that type and keep ingredients gentle.

Morning skincare routine for sensitive skin

  1. Rinse or cleanse gently. Lukewarm water alone is fine in the morning. If you need a cleanser, choose a low-foam, fragrance-free one.
  2. Moisturise. A simple cream or gel-cream with few ingredients. Pat it on; don't rub.
  3. Sunscreen. SPF 30 or higher. Many sensitive faces do better with mineral filters (zinc oxide or titanium dioxide). Patch test sunscreen too.

That is it. Skip toners with alcohol, face mists with perfume and morning actives until your skin feels calm.

Night routine for sensitive skin

  1. Take off sunscreen gently. A cleansing balm or micellar water on cotton, pressed (not scrubbed), then a mild cleanser if needed.
  2. Pat dry with a soft, clean towel. Keep a separate towel for your face.
  3. Moisturise. The same cream as the morning, or a slightly richer one. Our night cream for sensitive skin guide explains how to pick one.
  4. Optional extra. On irritated nights, a thin layer of a plain barrier balm on red or dry spots only.

Panthenol cream for sensitive skin

Panthenol is a form of vitamin B5. In skincare it acts as a humectant, drawing water into the skin, and is widely used in creams for dry, tight or easily irritated skin because it helps skin feel soothed and moisturised. A panthenol cream for sensitive skin is a sensible option if your face often feels uncomfortable after cleansing.

To be clear: no Mirc Glow cream contains panthenol. We include it here because it is a common, well-tolerated ingredient worth knowing about. Look for it in a short, fragrance-free formula, and still patch test.

How to add a new product safely

  1. One new product at a time. Wait at least 2 weeks before adding another.
  2. Patch test first. Apply a small amount behind the ear or on the jawline once a day for 3–5 days.
  3. Start slowly. Use it every second or third day for the first week.
  4. Stop if it stings, itches or causes bumps that last, and go back to your basic routine.

Actives like niacinamide or kojic acid can be fine for some sensitive skin, but start them only once your skin is calm, and one at a time. Our guide on kojic acid side effects explains what mild tingling vs a real reaction looks like.

Sensitive skin through Indian seasons

  • Summer: sweat and heat cause flushing. Use lighter textures, rinse sweat off with water, and keep sunscreen on. See avocado cream for sensitive skin in summer.
  • Monsoon: humidity plus damp towels can cause bumps. Change towels and pillow covers often.
  • Winter: dry air makes skin sting. Switch to a slightly richer cream and avoid hot water.

Short answers

How many steps should a sensitive skin routine have? Three in the morning (cleanse, moisturise, sunscreen) and two or three at night is plenty.

Is panthenol good for sensitive skin? It is generally well tolerated and helps skin feel moisturised and soothed, but patch test any new product.

Can sensitive skin use niacinamide? Many people can. Start once your skin is calm, use it every other day at first and stop if it stings.

Should I avoid fragrance with sensitive skin? It is a sensible first step, because fragrance is a common cause of irritation.

How long should I patch test? Once a day for 3–5 days on a small area before using a product on your whole face.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
