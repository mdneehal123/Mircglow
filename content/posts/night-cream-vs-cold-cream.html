Night cream vs cold cream comes down to one thing: a cold cream is mainly a protective layer that holds moisture in, while a night cream moisturises and usually carries a few active ingredients meant to work while you sleep. Cold cream is brilliant for very dry hands, cracked heels and windburnt cheeks. A night cream is the better everyday choice for your face.

Plenty of Indian homes keep a tub of cold cream from November to February. Here's how the two differ, who should use which, and how to use both without a greasy face.

What cold cream actually is

Cold cream is one of the oldest skincare formulas around. The classic recipe is water whipped into oils and waxes, which gives a thick, white, rich cream. Modern versions often use mineral oil, petrolatum or waxes such as beeswax, sometimes with glycerin and a fragrance. The name comes from the cool feeling it leaves when the water in it evaporates on skin.

What it does well is sit on top. The oils and waxes form a film that slows water loss from the skin, which is exactly what you want on a cold, dry Delhi morning when your knuckles are splitting.

What it doesn't do much of is anything beyond that. Most cold creams aren't built around ingredients like niacinamide or kojic acid. And on the face, a heavy occlusive layer can feel suffocating for oily or acne-prone skin, especially once you're indoors with a heater on.

What a night cream is

A night cream is a face moisturiser made for evening use. It's usually richer than a day cream but lighter than a cold cream, so it sinks in instead of sitting on the surface all night. The bigger difference is what's inside. Many night creams include ingredients that help skin look smoother and more even over weeks, such as niacinamide (vitamin B3), kojic acid, glutathione or hyaluronic acid.

Night is a good time for these because you're not in the sun, not sweating, and not touching your face for hours. Who should use which

Cold cream makes sense if

  • Your skin is very dry and flaky, and lighter creams vanish within an hour.
  • You're dealing with chapped cheeks after a bike ride in the cold, or a trip to Shimla or Manali.
  • Your face is calm, not acne-prone, and you just want comfort, no actives.

A night cream makes more sense if

  • Your skin is normal, combination or oily. Cold cream will likely feel too heavy.
  • You'd like help with dullness, uneven-looking tone or rough texture, not just dryness.
  • You get small bumps from heavy products.
  • You live somewhere winter is mild, like Bengaluru, Chennai or Kochi. Cold cream rarely makes sense there for the face.

Honestly, if your face is extremely dry and sensitive and a plain cold cream keeps it comfortable, you don't need to switch. A night cream with actives isn't automatically better. It's just doing a different job.

Can you use both?

Yes, and a lot of people in north India already do without thinking about it. The trick is to give each one its own area.

  1. Wash your face at night and pat it almost dry.
  2. Apply a pea-sized amount of night cream on the face and neck. Our guide on how to apply night cream covers the technique.
  3. Use cold cream on hands, knuckles, elbows, heels and lips (if it's fragrance-free).
  4. On very dry patches on the face, like the sides of the nose or around the mouth, you can dab a tiny bit of cold cream over the night cream. Just on those spots, not the whole face.

Night cream always goes first, cold cream on top. A heavy occlusive layer underneath would stop a lighter cream from settling in. Skip it as a full-face top coat if you're prone to pimples. See dry patches on face if you get flaky spots every winter.

Winter tips for north India

Delhi, Lucknow, Chandigarh and Jaipur winters are dry, and indoor heaters make them drier. A few small habits matter as much as which cream you pick.

  • Wash with lukewarm water, not hot. Hot water strips skin fast, and hard water makes it worse.
  • Put cream on within a minute or two of washing, while skin is still slightly damp.
  • If you sleep with a heater or blower on, keep a bucket of water or a humidifier in the room.
  • Switch to a richer night cream from November, and back to a lighter one in March.
  • Keep sunscreen going in the morning. Winter sun on a rooftop or during a cricket match still counts.

Short answers

Is cold cream a night cream? Not really. Cold cream is a heavy protective moisturiser; a night cream is made for the face at night and often includes active ingredients.

Can I apply cold cream on my face at night? Yes, if your skin is very dry and not acne-prone. Oily or pimple-prone skin usually finds it too heavy.

Night cream vs cold cream for winter, which is better? For the face, a rich night cream for most people. For hands, heels and chapped areas, cold cream.

Can I put cold cream over night cream? On small dry patches, yes. Night cream first, a thin dab of cold cream on top.

Do I need sunscreen after using night cream? Yes, every morning, especially if your night cream contains kojic acid.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
