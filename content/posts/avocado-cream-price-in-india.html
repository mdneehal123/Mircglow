What drives avocado cream price in India?

Two creams with "avocado" on the front can cost very different amounts. The main reasons are:

  • Size. A 10 g jar and a 50 g jar are not the same product, even if the box looks similar.
  • Ingredients. Avocado alone costs less to make than avocado plus niacinamide, kojic acid and other supporting ingredients.
  • Packaging. Glass jars, airless pumps and heavy outer boxes add cost. Simple, sealed jars cost less.
  • Brand costs. Advertising, celebrity faces and shop rent all end up in the price you pay.
  • Shipping and taxes. GST is included in MRP in India. Some sellers also add delivery charges at checkout.

Why price per gram matters most

The easiest mistake is to compare only the sticker price. A smaller jar can look cheaper and still cost you much more over a month.

To work out price per gram, divide the price by the number of grams. For example, ₹649 ÷ 30 g = about ₹21.6 per gram. Now you can compare any two jars fairly, whatever their size.

Also check whether the size is in grams (g) or millilitres (ml). Creams are usually sold by weight. If one is in ml, the comparison is roughly similar, but not exact.

How long does a jar last?

This is the other half of value. A pea-sized amount for the face, plus a little for the neck, is roughly 0.3 to 0.5 g per use. Using that as a rough guide:

  • A 10 g jar lasts about 3 to 4 weeks of nightly use.
  • A 25–30 g jar lasts about 2 to 3 months of nightly use.

Your own use will vary. Dry skin in a north Indian winter needs more. Oily skin in summer needs less. So the bigger jar is usually the better buy once you know the cream suits you.

When a cheaper cream is not the better deal

Very low prices are not always good value. Watch for:

  • Tiny jar sizes printed in small letters.
  • No full ingredient list, so you cannot tell how much avocado is really in it.
  • No expiry date or batch number.
  • Big promises about changing your skin colour or overnight change. A cosmetic cream helps skin feel moisturised and look more even over weeks, not days.
  • Delivery charges that only show at checkout.

When paying more makes sense

A higher price can be fair when it buys you something real:

  • More useful ingredients, such as niacinamide or kojic acid alongside avocado.
  • A larger jar that lowers the cost per gram.
  • A sealed jar, clear labelling and a proper returns policy.

Price alone does not tell you how a cream will suit your skin. A patch test and a few weeks of steady use tell you far more.
