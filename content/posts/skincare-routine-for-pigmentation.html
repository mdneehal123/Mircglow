A good skincare routine for pigmentation is simple: a gentle cleanser, a spot-focused moisturiser at night, and sunscreen every morning, repeated for 8 to 12 weeks. That steady routine helps dark spots, tan marks and patches look less noticeable and gives you a more even-looking complexion over time.

Most people with pigmentation-prone skin do too much, not too little. They scrub, switch creams every two weeks and forget sunscreen on cloudy days. Below is a calm morning and night routine built for Indian weather, plus the habits that protect your progress.

How to care for pigmentation-prone skin: the basics

Pigmentation means some areas of skin make more melanin than the skin around them. Sun, heat, old pimples, rubbing, waxing burns and hormones can all trigger it. Indian skin tends to make extra pigment easily, so the gentlest routine is usually the best one.

  • Protect first. UV rays and heat keep telling skin to make pigment. Sunscreen is not optional.
  • Calm, do not attack. Irritation leaves new marks. Avoid harsh scrubs, lemon, toothpaste and undiluted home remedies.
  • Be patient. Skin renews roughly every 4 to 6 weeks, so a more even look needs at least two cycles.

Morning skincare routine for uneven skin tone

  1. Rinse or cleanse. Plain water is enough for dry skin; oily skin can use a mild face wash.
  2. Light moisturiser. A pea-sized amount, or half that in humid weather. A light cream with niacinamide suits most skin types in the day.
  3. Sunscreen. SPF 30 or higher, broad spectrum. Use about two finger-lengths for the face and neck.
  4. Reapply when outdoors. Every 2 to 3 hours if you are outside, sweating or commuting by two-wheeler. A cap, dupatta or umbrella helps a lot.

Night skincare routine for dark spots

Night is the best time for spot-focused ingredients, because there is no sun undoing the work.

  1. Cleanse well. Wash off sunscreen, sweat and pollution with a gentle face wash. Double cleanse only if you wore heavy makeup.
  2. Pat dry. Do not rub with a rough towel.
  3. Apply a spot-focused cream. Look for kojic acid (typically about 1–2%), alpha arbutin, niacinamide or vitamin C. Use a pea-sized amount over the whole face, not just on the spots, because new patches often appear next to old ones.
  4. Extra moisture if needed. Dry areas can take a thin second layer of a plain moisturiser.

Sunscreen: where every skincare routine for pigmentation starts

In any sunscreen and pigmentation skincare routine, this is the step that matters most. If you only change one habit, make it daily sunscreen. Without it, new pigment forms faster than any cream can keep up with.

Skincare routine for uneven complexion by skin type

Oily or acne-prone skin

Keep textures light. A niacinamide moisturiser at night and a gel sunscreen in the day is often enough. Do not pick pimples; that is how most marks start. See night cream for oily skin.

Dry skin

Dry, flaky skin looks more uneven than it is. A richer cream, applied on slightly damp skin, makes the surface look smoother and more even. See avocado cream for dry skin.

Sensitive skin

Start your spot-focused cream every second night after a patch test. Alpha arbutin and niacinamide are often gentler than kojic acid.

Weekly habits that support a more even look

  • Exfoliate gently at most once or twice a week, never with gritty scrubs on active pimples.
  • Change pillowcases twice a week, especially in humid months.
  • Avoid hot water on the face; lukewarm is kinder.
  • Do not stack many new actives at once. Add one product at a time and give it four weeks.
  • Take a photo in the same light every two weeks. Changes are slow and easy to miss in the mirror.

When to see a dermatologist

  • Large, symmetrical brown patches on the cheeks, forehead or upper lip, which may be melasma.
  • Spots that change shape, colour or size, bleed or itch.
  • Pigmentation that started with a new medicine, pregnancy or a rash.
  • Painful or cystic acne that keeps leaving new marks.

Short answers

What is the best skincare routine for pigmentation? Gentle cleanser, a spot-focused moisturiser at night and sunscreen every morning, followed steadily for 8 to 12 weeks.

Should I use my dark spot cream in the morning or at night? Night is easiest. If you use it in the morning, always finish with sunscreen.

Can I skip sunscreen if I stay indoors? It is better not to. Window light and short trips outside still add up.

How many products do I need? Three is enough: cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. Add more only if your skin is calm.

Is pigmentation on Indian skin harder to manage? It can be slower, because Indian skin makes pigment easily, so gentle care and patience matter even more.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
