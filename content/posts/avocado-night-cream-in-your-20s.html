The secret in your 20s is not doing more. It is doing a few basic steps every day, and protecting your skin from the sun. Here is how a night cream fits in.

Do you really need a night cream in your 20s?

Not everyone does. If your skin feels comfortable after washing at night, a light moisturiser may be enough. But many people in their 20s notice:

  • Skin that feels tight after face wash, especially in hostels or offices with AC.
  • Rough or flaky patches on the cheeks in winter.
  • Marks left behind by old pimples.
  • A dull look after exams, night shifts or a lot of screen time.
  • Uneven-looking patches from the sun on two-wheeler rides or commutes.

If any of these sound familiar, a night cream is a sensible step. It is also a good habit to build early, because the routine you start now is the one you are most likely to keep.

A simple routine for your 20s

At night

  1. Wash off sunscreen, makeup and dust with a gentle face wash.
  2. Pat dry, leaving the skin slightly damp.
  3. Apply a pea-sized amount of night cream, or half that if your skin is oily.
  4. Avoid the eye area, and include the neck.

In the morning

  1. Rinse your face with water or a mild face wash.
  2. Use a light moisturiser if your skin feels dry. Our avocado day cream guide explains how.
  3. Finish with a broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher. This is the most important step.

Habits that matter more than any cream

  • Sunscreen every day. Sun is the biggest reason skin looks uneven and tired later. Starting in your 20s is the best gift to your future skin.
  • Sleep. Late nights show on the face. Even one extra hour helps.
  • Do not pick pimples. Picking is what leaves most dark marks behind.
  • Change pillow covers often. Once or twice a week is a good rule, especially if you are acne-prone.
  • Wash your face after the gym or a sweaty commute.
  • Keep it simple. Social media trends push many products at once. Add one new product at a time and give it a few weeks.

Mistakes to avoid in your 20s

  • Using too many actives at once, such as kojic acid, strong acids and retinoids together.
  • Expecting overnight change. Skin renews roughly every 4–6 weeks, so a more even look takes 8–12 weeks.
  • Trying home hacks like lemon, toothpaste or baking soda on the face.
  • Buying cheap, unlabelled creams that promise very quick change.
  • Skipping sunscreen because you are "only going to college".

Patch test and when to see a dermatologist

Patch test any new cream behind your ear or on your inner arm for two nights before using it on your face. If kojic acid is new to you, start every second night.

See a dermatologist if your acne is painful, deep or cystic, if pimples keep leaving dark marks or pits, if you have a rash that does not settle, or if brown patches on both cheeks keep spreading. Short answers

Is avocado night cream good for young skin? Yes. It helps young skin feel moisturised overnight and suits a simple, low-effort routine.

At what age should I start a night cream? There is no fixed age. Start when your skin feels dry or tight at night, often in your late teens or 20s.

Can I use avocado night cream in my 20s if I have oily skin? Yes, in a thin layer at night. Half a pea-sized amount on the cheeks is usually enough.

Do I need anti-ageing products in my 20s? Daily sunscreen and a good moisturiser cover most needs at this age.

Can I use it with acne? Use a thin layer, skip active pimples, and see a dermatologist if acne is painful or cystic.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
