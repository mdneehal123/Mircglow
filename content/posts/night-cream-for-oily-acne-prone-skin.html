If your skin is oily and breakout-prone, it is tempting to skip night cream. But dry, over-cleansed skin can make more oil and feel worse. A light night cream used in a small amount can help keep skin comfortable while the rest of your routine does its work.

A short, gentle routine

  1. Cleanse twice a day at most. Use a mild face wash and lukewarm water.
  2. Treat only as directed. If you use a prescribed acne treatment, follow your dermatologist's instructions.
  3. Moisturise with a pea-sized amount. Press it in, and use less on the T-zone.
  4. Wear sunscreen in the morning. Sun makes marks left by pimples darker.

Introduce one new product at a time and give it a few weeks. Our guide on how to patch test a face cream shows how to test safely.

What not to do

  • Do not pick or squeeze pimples. It can leave marks that last longer.
  • Do not scrub hard or wash many times a day.
  • Do not use toothpaste, lemon or home remedies on pimples.
  • Do not stack lots of actives at once.

Can night cream cause pimples?

A cream that is too rich for your skin can feel congesting. If you notice new bumps after starting a product, stop and patch test again. We explain more in can night cream cause pimples.

Short answers

Should acne-prone skin use night cream? Usually yes, a light one. Skipping moisturiser can leave skin dehydrated and oilier.

Which ingredient helps oily skin? Niacinamide and aloe vera are common choices for a light, balanced feel.

Will night cream clear my acne? No. A night cream is a moisturiser, not a treatment for acne.

When should I see a dermatologist? If acne is painful, deep, scarring or has not improved after a few weeks of gentle care.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
