You know the feeling. You wash your face, pat it dry, and within a minute it feels a size too small. By evening there are fine flaky patches near the nose and mouth. That is dry skin, and it is exactly the skin type that avocado cream for dry skin was made for.

Here is why it works, and how to use it so you get the benefit and not just a greasy pillow.

Dry skin is short of oil, not just water

People often mix up two things. Dehydrated skin lacks water. Dry skin lacks oil. Your skin makes its own oils to hold moisture in, and dry skin simply makes less of them. Splashing on water or a light gel helps for ten minutes and then the tightness comes back, because nothing is holding that water in place.

So dry skin needs a cream that puts some richness back. That is where avocado comes in.

Why avocado suits dry skin

Avocado is one of the oiliest fruits there is, and the oil is high in oleic acid, the same fatty acid that makes olive oil feel so soft on the hands. In a cream, it gives a cushioned, comfortable layer that sits well on dry skin and takes away that stretched feeling.

It is not magic. It is a good, rich moisturiser. For dry skin, that is the thing you need most.

5 ways to get more out of it

Put it on damp skin

This one change makes the biggest difference. Do not wait until your face is bone dry. Pat off the dripping water, and apply the cream while your skin still feels slightly damp. The cream then seals that water in.

Use lukewarm water, not hot

A hot shower in winter feels wonderful and leaves dry skin worse. Hot water strips the little oil you have. Wash your face with lukewarm water and keep it short.

Make night your main application

Rich creams do their best work overnight, when nothing is competing with them. Use a full pea-sized amount on your face and a little extra on your neck, which gets dry too and is usually forgotten.

Press, do not rub

Warm the cream between your fingertips and press it into the skin. Rubbing hard just moves it around and can leave dry skin red.

Be generous on the dry patches

The sides of the nose, around the mouth and the cheeks usually need more. Go back over them with a second thin layer.

What to change with the seasons

In winter, especially in the north, dry skin needs the full amount every night, and often a light layer in the morning under sunscreen as well.

In summer and during the monsoon, the air carries more moisture. Cut back to a thinner layer at night. If you spend the day in air conditioning, keep using it, because cooled air is very drying.

Things that quietly make dry skin worse

  • Bar soap on the face.
  • Foaming face washes that leave skin squeaky.
  • Scrubbing off flakes. It feels satisfying and leaves skin rawer.
  • Toners with a lot of alcohol.
  • Skipping cream because it is hot outside.

How soon will it feel better?

The tightness usually eases the first night. Flaky patches take about a week of steady use to settle. After that it is simply a matter of not stopping. Dry skin does not get cured. It gets looked after, a little every night.
