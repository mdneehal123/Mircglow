If you are wondering how to introduce active ingredients into skincare safely, the short answer is: add one new active at a time, patch test it first, start two or three times a week, and wait 2–4 weeks before adding anything else. Going slowly is how you avoid irritation and actually learn what works for your skin.

This guide explains what counts as an active, a simple week-by-week plan, which ingredients to keep apart, and how to layer products for dark spots without overloading your skin.

What counts as an active ingredient

"Actives" are ingredients added for a specific job beyond basic moisture. Common ones include:

  • Brightening-focused: kojic acid, alpha arbutin, glutathione, vitamin C
  • Barrier and balance: niacinamide (vitamin B3)
  • Texture: retinol, exfoliating acids such as AHAs and BHAs

Moisturisers like aloe vera, avocado, glycerin and hyaluronic acid are usually gentle and are not what people mean by strong actives. For short notes on each, see the skincare ingredient glossary.

How to introduce active ingredients into skincare: a 6-week plan

  1. Before you start: make sure your basics are steady for at least two weeks. That means a gentle cleanser, a moisturiser and sunscreen every morning.
  2. Patch test: apply the new product behind the ear or on the jawline for two or three days. No redness, itching or burning? Move on.
  3. Weeks 1–2: use it two or three nights a week. Plain moisturiser on other nights.
  4. Weeks 3–4: if skin feels comfortable, go to every other night.
  5. Weeks 5–6: move to nightly use if the product is meant for it and your skin is happy.
  6. Only then consider adding a second active, starting the whole process again.

Expect results slowly. Skin renews roughly every 4–6 weeks, so a more even look usually takes 8–12 weeks of steady use.

How to layer products for dark spots

When people want to reduce the look of dark spots, the temptation is to pile on serum after serum. A simpler routine usually works better and irritates less:

Night

  1. Gentle cleanser.
  2. Hydrating serum (optional), such as hyaluronic acid.
  3. One brightening-focused night cream.

Morning

  1. Rinse or gentle cleanser.
  2. Light moisturiser.
  3. Broad-spectrum sunscreen. This is the step that protects all your night-time effort.

For more on spots specifically, read kojic acid cream for dark spots and how to use glutathione cream.

How to layer moisturiser and serum

Go from thinnest to thickest: serum on clean skin first, then moisturiser or night cream on top to hold it in. Wait until the serum no longer feels wet, usually under a minute. Our full guide on how to layer serum and night cream covers exceptions such as oils and thick serums.

Signs you are going too fast

  • Stinging when you apply even a plain moisturiser
  • Tightness, flaking or a shiny, "stretched" look
  • New redness or small bumps
  • Products that used to feel fine suddenly sting

If this happens, stop all actives for a week or two and use only a gentle cleanser, a plain moisturiser and sunscreen. Then restart one product at a lower frequency. Our avocado night cream side effects guide explains what mild reactions can look like.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist if you have painful or cystic acne, melasma, a spreading rash, or irritation that does not settle after stopping products. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before starting retinol or any new active.

Short answers

How many actives should a beginner use? One at a time. Once your skin is settled after a few weeks, you can consider a second.

How often should I use a new active? Start with two or three nights a week and increase slowly if your skin is comfortable.

Can I use kojic acid and retinol together? Beginners should use them on different nights, at least for the first several weeks.

What order do serum and moisturiser go in? Serum first, then moisturiser or night cream on top.

Do I need sunscreen with actives? Yes, every morning, especially with kojic acid, vitamin C, alpha arbutin or retinol.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
