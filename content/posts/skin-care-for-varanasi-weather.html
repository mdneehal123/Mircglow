Skin care for Varanasi weather means protecting skin from a very hot, dry summer with loo winds from about April to June, keeping it light and clean through the humid monsoon from late June to September, and moisturising well through the cold, foggy winter from December to February. Gentle cleansing and broad-spectrum sunscreen stay the same all year; only the weight of your moisturiser and night cream should change.

Varanasi sits on the Ganga in eastern Uttar Pradesh, far from the sea. Long hours on the ghats, boat rides and winter fog all affect skin, so here is a routine built around the city's year.

A daily skin care routine for Varanasi

Morning

  1. Gentle face wash with lukewarm water.

Night

Varanasi summer: loo, sun on the ghats and dust

From April to June, the stone steps of the ghats and the open river reflect a lot of sun, and the afternoon loo is hot and dry. Skin tans quickly and can feel dry and oily at the same time.

  • Plan ghat walks, temple visits and boat rides for early morning or after sunset when you can.
  • Wear a cap, carry an umbrella or cover your head with a dupatta or gamchha outdoors.
  • Reapply sunscreen after a few hours outside or after heavy sweating; a boat ride on the open river gives no shade at all.
  • Rinse dust and sweat off with plain water when you get home instead of leaving it on for hours.
  • Keep a light moisturiser even in the heat. Dry wind dehydrates skin, which can make it look dull and shiny at once.

For a hot, tight face after a long day out, our after-sun skincare guide has simple steps, and skincare for bike riders helps if you commute on a scooter. Tan fades slowly over several weeks with shade and daily sunscreen; no cream removes it overnight.

Monsoon in Varanasi: late June to September

The rains make the air heavy and humid, the river rises, and some lanes stay wet and muddy for days. Expect greasiness, clogged pores, sweat bumps and itching where skin rubs together.

  • Switch to lighter textures and use about half your usual night cream if your face feels greasy.
  • If you walk through dirty water in the lanes, wash your feet and legs with soap and clean water as soon as you can, and dry between the toes.

Varanasi winter: fog, cold mornings and heaters

December and January bring dense fog and cold-wave spells to eastern UP. Hot baths, room heaters and early-morning dips or aarti in the cold all dry out skin, so start winter care in late November.

  1. Keep bath water warm, not very hot, and keep baths short.
  2. Pat skin half-dry and moisturise within about three minutes, while it is still slightly damp.
  3. Use a full pea-sized night cream on the cheeks and around the nose, and lip balm before bed.
  4. Rub a thick cream or a little oil into hands and heels at night and wear socks; cracked heels are common in winter.
  5. Keep sunscreen on, especially for rooftop afternoons and Makar Sankranti kite flying in January.

River dips, festivals and long days on the ghats

Many people take a dip in the Ganga or spend hours at the ghats during festivals. River water can leave skin feeling dry or slightly itchy for some people. Rinse with clean tap water afterwards if you can, pat dry and moisturise. Skip active creams such as kojic acid that night if your skin feels sore.

Festival evenings mean smoke from diyas and incense and late nights, so cleanse gently before bed even when tired. After Holi, take colour off with a little oil and a gentle cleanser instead of scrubbing, and give your skin a day or two of plain moisturiser before returning to actives.

Hard water, dust and smoke

Many homes use borewell or tank water that can feel hard and leave skin tight after washing. A low-foam face wash and a moisturiser straight afterwards help; our hard water skincare guide explains more. On dusty and hazy days, cleanse well at night and skip harsh scrubs. Varanasi vs Lucknow and Patna

All three cities share the north Indian plains climate, with hot dry summers and foggy winters. Our Lucknow weather guide and Patna weather guide are useful companion reads. What stands out in Varanasi is how much time many people spend outdoors on the river and the ghats, so shade, sunscreen reapplication and a proper cleanse at night matter most here.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for sunburn with blisters, painful or cystic acne, prickly heat that becomes infected, a spreading itchy rash in skin folds during the monsoon, cracked skin that bleeds or does not get better, or patches of colour change that keep growing. If you feel dizzy or very unwell in the heat, get medical help straight away. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before using creams with active ingredients.

Short answers

What is the best skin care routine for Varanasi weather? A gentle face wash, a moisturiser, broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning and a night cream at bedtime. Go lighter from April to September and richer from November to February.

How do I protect my skin on the ghats in summer? Go early or after sunset, cover your head, reapply sunscreen every few hours and rinse off sweat and dust when you get home.

Why is my skin so dry in Varanasi winter? Cold, dry, foggy air plus hot baths and heaters pull water from the skin. Shorter warm baths, moisturiser on damp skin and a richer night cream help.

Should I wash my face after a Ganga dip? If your skin feels dry or itchy afterwards, rinse with clean water, pat dry and moisturise, and skip active creams that night.
