If your face is shiny by lunchtime, putting more cream on it at night sounds like a terrible idea. Plenty of people with oily skin skip moisturiser for exactly that reason. It usually backfires.

Oil and moisture are not the same thing. Oily skin can still be short of water, and skin that has been dried out often looks shinier, not less. The right night cream for oily skin, used the right way, leaves it calmer and more balanced. Here are six rules that make it work.

Does oily skin really need a night cream?

Yes. Washing your face takes off oil, sweat and sunscreen. It takes off moisture too. If nothing goes back on, your skin feels tight, and by the next afternoon it looks shinier than ever. A light layer of cream at night keeps it comfortable and smoother-looking by morning.

6 rules for a night cream for oily skin

1. Use less than you think you need

This is the one that matters. Half a pea for the whole face. Most of what people blame on a cream comes down to using too much of it.

2. Put it where you need it

Oily skin is hardly ever oily all over. Cheeks and jaw tend to need more than the forehead and nose. Start with the drier parts and use whatever is left on your fingers for the rest.

3. Wash properly first

If you wore sunscreen or make-up, cleanse twice in the evening: an oil-based cleanser, then a gentle face wash. Cream on top of leftover sunscreen and dust is what blocks pores.

4. Look for ingredients that help

  • Niacinamide (vitamin B3). A favourite for a smoother, less shiny look.
  • Aloe vera. Light and fresh.
  • Hyaluronic acid. Hydration with no heaviness.
  • Kojic acid. For the look of marks left by pimples.

5. Give it ten minutes

Let the cream settle before your face meets the pillow. It absorbs better, and your pillowcase stays clean.

6. Change with the weather

In humid months, use the thinnest layer you can manage. In winter, or if you sleep with the AC on, oily skin often wants a bit more.

Can oily skin use a rich cream?

It can, as long as you follow the first two rules. A rich cream in a very thin layer often does better than a watery gel slapped on thickly. What to stay away from

  • Skipping moisturiser completely.
  • Washing your face more than twice a day.
  • Harsh scrubs and toners loaded with alcohol.
  • Going to bed in sunscreen or make-up.

A night routine that takes two minutes

  1. Oil cleanser, then a gel face wash.
  2. A light, alcohol-free toner.
  3. Half a pea of night cream, mostly on the cheeks.
