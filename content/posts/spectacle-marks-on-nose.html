Spectacle marks on the nose are usually dents or darker patches where the nose pads press and rub every day, and the most useful step is to reduce that pressure and friction: get your frame adjusted, keep the pads and skin clean, and moisturise the area. With the pressure gone, gentle daily care and sunscreen help the mark look softer over weeks to months, but how much it fades differs from person to person.

Many Indians wear glasses from school age, often in heat and humidity where sweat and oil collect under the pads. Below is what causes these marks, a simple routine for the nose area, what to expect, and when to see a doctor.

Step 1: Fix the fit first

No cream can do much while the same spot is pressed for 12 hours a day. Visit your optician and ask for:

  • A frame adjustment so the weight sits more evenly on the ears and nose.
  • Soft silicone pads or larger pads that spread the pressure.
  • Lighter frames or thinner lenses if your power is high and the glasses are heavy.
  • A different pad position, so it rests on a slightly new spot.

Give your nose short breaks during the day when you do not need to see far, for example while resting your eyes. If you use contact lenses on some days, follow your eye doctor's advice on how long to wear them.

Step 2: A simple daily routine for the nose area

  1. Clean the pads daily with mild soap and water or a lens wipe. Oil and sweat build up fast in Indian summers and monsoons.
  2. Wash your face gently morning and night, without scrubbing the marks.
  3. Moisturise the sides of the nose. Skin that is soft and comfortable copes better with friction than dry, rough skin.
  4. Apply sunscreen every morning on the whole face, including the nose and the skin under the pads, and reapply if you are out in the sun.
  5. At night, use your usual night cream on the face, including the marked areas, after the glasses come off.

Step 3: For darker-looking marks, be gentle and patient

A spot that has been rubbed for years can look darker than the skin around it. Once the friction is lower, skin tends to look more even slowly, because the top layer renews roughly every 4 to 6 weeks and a more even look usually takes 8 to 12 weeks or longer.

Spectacle marks on the nose and under the eyes

Some people notice marks or dryness under the eyes too, where the lower rim touches or where they rub their eyes when taking glasses off. The skin there is thin, so use only a gentle cream and keep it away from the lash line. When to see a doctor

See a dermatologist if the mark is sore, raised, bleeding, keeps getting infected, or is growing or changing in colour or shape. A small lump, or a sore on the nose that does not go away, should always be checked. If the frame pads cause itching or a rash, you may be reacting to the material; ask a doctor and your optician.

Short answers

Do spectacle marks on the nose go away? Marks from pressure often soften once the frame fits better and the skin gets less friction. Darker patches from years of rubbing can take months to look more even, and some stay partly visible.

What should I apply on spectacle marks? A gentle moisturiser daily, sunscreen every morning, and if you like, a night cream with ingredients such as niacinamide or kojic acid after a patch test.

Are home remedies like lemon or potato good for glasses marks? Lemon can irritate the thin skin on the nose, so skip it. Potato or aloe vera is gentler, but the most useful step is fixing the frame fit.

Can silicone nose pads help? Yes, softer or larger pads spread the pressure and can reduce the dent and rubbing. Ask your optician to fit them.

Should I stop wearing glasses to let the mark fade? No. Wear the glasses you need and ask your eye doctor before switching to contact lenses.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
