Serum before or after night cream? Before. The general rule is thinnest to thickest: a watery serum goes on clean skin first, and the night cream goes on top to hold it in.

There are a few exceptions, and a few habits that make layering feel sticky or cause little balls of product to roll off in humid weather. Here is the full picture.

Serum before or after night cream: why thin goes first

Serums are light and mostly water-based. You use only a few drops, and they are meant to sit close to the skin. Night creams contain oils and butters that form a soft layer over the skin and slow down moisture loss while you sleep.

If the cream goes on first, the serum ends up sitting on top of that layer and spreads poorly. It is a bit like wearing a raincoat under your kurta. Everything is technically on, just in the wrong order.

The basic night order

  1. Take off makeup and sunscreen with micellar water or a cleansing balm.
  2. Wash your face with a gentle face wash and pat dry.
  3. Toner, if you use one. It is optional.
  4. Serum, two or three drops pressed into the skin.
  5. Eye cream, if you use one, tapped on lightly.
  6. Night cream, a pea-sized amount for the face, a little more for the neck.
  7. Lip balm to finish.

How long to wait between them

Usually 30 to 60 seconds, just until the serum no longer feels wet. There is no need to wait ten or twenty minutes. On sticky monsoon evenings skin may stay tacky for longer. Sitting under a fan for a minute, or using one drop less, helps.

The one exception is a prescription product. If a doctor has given you a cream or gel with specific timing, follow their instructions over any general rule.

When the order changes

Facial oils

Oils go last, after the night cream, or you can mix a drop into the cream in your palm. Oil first would make it harder for anything else to spread.

Thick or creamy serums

Go by texture, not by name. If a product called a serum feels like a lotion, it goes after a watery one, not before it.

Sleeping masks

These go on top of the night cream or replace it on that night. Using both every night is usually more than skin needs.

Do you even need a serum?

Honestly, not always. If your night cream already has active ingredients, a serum is optional.

  • A serum may help if your skin feels dehydrated in winter or after long hours under the AC, or if you have one clear goal, such as a smoother look.
  • You can skip it if you have oily skin and layers feel heavy in summer, if your skin is sensitive, or if you would rather keep your routine short and affordable.

Common layering mistakes

  • Too much product. Two or three drops of serum and a pea-sized amount of cream cover the face.
  • Rubbing hard. Press and smooth instead. Heavy rubbing is a common reason for pilling, those small balls that roll off the skin.
  • Not waiting at all. Applying cream onto a soaking-wet serum can also cause pilling.
  • Too many actives. One brightening or exfoliating product per night is plenty.
  • Skipping cream in summer. A thin layer is better than none, especially if your serum contains actives.
  • Trying several new products at once. Patch test each new serum or cream separately so you know which one your skin is reacting to.

Adjusting for Indian weather

In summer and monsoon, choose a gel serum and a thin layer of a lighter night cream. In winter, or if you sleep with the AC on, apply a hydrating serum to slightly damp skin and follow with a richer cream. If hard water leaves your face feeling tight, apply your serum and cream soon after washing rather than waiting.

Whatever the season, if your night cream contains kojic acid, wear sunscreen the next morning. And if layering ever leads to burning, a rash or painful breakouts, stop and see a dermatologist.

Short answers

Do I apply serum before night cream? Yes. Serum goes on clean skin first, and night cream goes on top.

How long should I wait between them? Until the serum no longer feels wet, usually under a minute.

Can I use two serums at night? Yes, thinnest first, but keep it to one active serum per night.

Can I mix serum into my night cream? You can with a simple hydrating serum, but layering lets each product spread as intended.

Where does face oil go? Last, after the night cream, or mixed into it.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
