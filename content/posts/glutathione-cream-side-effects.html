It is a fair thing to ask before you buy. Are there glutathione cream side effects, and should you worry? The short answer is that they are uncommon and usually mild. The longer answer is worth two minutes of your time.

This article is about creams you put on your skin. Tablets and injections are a different subject with different risks, and only a doctor should advise you on those. What follows is general information and not medical advice.

Is glutathione cream safe?

Glutathione is something your own body produces, and in a cream it is generally well tolerated. Here is the part most articles leave out: when someone does react to a glutathione cream, the cause is often another ingredient in the jar. Kojic acid, vitamin C, a fragrance or a preservative is more likely to be responsible than the glutathione itself.

Studies of glutathione used on the skin are few and small. Nobody can honestly say it is risk-free for every person, which is why a careful start matters.

4 glutathione cream side effects that can happen

1. Mild irritation

A bit of redness, warmth or itching in the first few uses. A quick tingle that fades is normal. Burning, or redness that hangs around, is your cue to stop.

2. Dryness or light flaking

Creams that include vitamin C or kojic acid can leave some skin a little dry at first. Dropping to every second night and keeping the rest of your routine gentle usually sorts it out.

3. An allergic reaction

Any cosmetic can cause an allergy in a small number of people. Watch for a rash, swelling, hives or strong itching. Wash the cream off and get medical help.

4. Skin that needs more sun protection

Creams for a brighter, more even-looking tone go hand in hand with sunscreen. Leave it out, and your skin can end up looking more uneven than when you started.

Who should be careful?

  • Anyone with eczema, rosacea, or cut or broken skin.
  • People already using prescription creams.
  • Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding. Please check with your doctor before starting any new skincare.
  • Anyone whose skin has reacted to products before.

Do a patch test

  1. Put a little cream behind your ear or on your inner forearm.
  2. Wait a full day.
  3. If the skin looks and feels normal, you are fine to use it on your face.

How to keep the risk low

  • Begin with every second night for two weeks.
  • A pea-sized amount is plenty. More cream does not mean more glow.
  • Keep strong exfoliating acids and retinoids for other nights until your skin has settled.
  • Sunscreen, every morning.
  • Buy from the brand or an authorised seller, and look for the batch number and use-before date on the pack.

When to stop

If burning, redness or itching lasts more than a day, or you see a rash or swelling, stop. If it is severe or does not calm down, see a doctor.

A warning about fast-result creams

This matters more than anything above. Some creams sold as quick skin whiteners have been found to contain steroids or mercury, which are not allowed in cosmetics and can do real harm. Stay away from anything that promises a dramatic change in days, has no ingredient list, or does not say who made it.
