What retinol is

Retinol is a form of vitamin A. In skincare it is one of the best-known ingredients for helping skin look smoother and more even over time. It speeds up the skin's natural renewal, which is why it can also cause dryness, flaking and redness in the first few weeks.

Retinol is part of a family called retinoids. Stronger retinoids are prescription-only, and those should be used only as your doctor advises.

How to start an anti-aging (retinol) night cream as a beginner

  1. Pick a low strength. Beginner products are often around 0.1–0.3% retinol. Higher is not better when you are starting.
  2. Patch test. Apply a little behind the ear or on the jawline for two or three nights first.
  3. Start with two nights a week, for example Monday and Thursday. On other nights, use a plain moisturiser.
  4. Apply to dry skin. Wait a few minutes after washing. Damp skin can increase irritation.
  5. Use a pea-sized amount for the whole face. Keep away from the corners of the eyes, nose and lips.
  6. Increase slowly. If skin is comfortable after 2–3 weeks, try three nights a week, then every other night.
  7. Wear sunscreen every morning. Retinol can make skin more sensitive to the sun.

Retinol cream for mature skin

A retinol cream for mature skin can be a good choice because mature skin often wants a smoother, more even look. But mature skin also tends to be drier, so irritation can show up faster.

  • Choose a creamy texture rather than a watery serum.
  • Try the "sandwich" method: a thin layer of moisturiser, then retinol, then more moisturiser.
  • On non-retinol nights, use a rich, comforting night cream. Our guide to avocado cream for dry skin explains why plant oils feel so soothing.

Retinol vs peptides for skincare

Peptides are short chains of amino acids used in creams to support firmer, smoother-looking skin. Compared with retinol:

  • Retinol has more history and research behind it, but it is harder on sensitive skin.
  • Peptides are generally gentle and suit people who cannot tolerate retinol, though results vary between products.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Starting too strong. A higher percentage usually means more irritation, not faster results.
  • Using it every night from day one. Skin needs a few weeks to adjust.
  • Adding retinol and other new actives together. If your skin reacts, you will not know which product caused it.
  • Skipping moisturiser. A good night cream on non-retinol nights keeps skin comfortable.
  • Forgetting sunscreen, especially in Indian summers.

Signs to slow down

  • Stinging that lasts more than a few minutes
  • Peeling that does not settle after a few days
  • Redness, burning or a rash

If these happen, stop for a week, use only a gentle cleanser and moisturiser, then restart less often. If irritation is severe, or you have rosacea, eczema or melasma, see a dermatologist before using retinol. Do not use retinol if you are pregnant or trying to conceive unless your doctor says it is fine.

Short answers

Can beginners use retinol every night? Not at first. Start with two nights a week and build up slowly over several weeks.

Do Mirc Glow creams contain retinol? No. Most of our creams contain niacinamide instead, a gentler ingredient.

Can I use retinol and niacinamide together? Usually yes. Many people use niacinamide on retinol nights or on alternate nights to keep skin comfortable.

Is retinol safe in summer? Yes, if you wear sunscreen every morning and reapply when outdoors.

How long does retinol take to show a change? Usually 8–12 weeks or more of steady use.
