What "pigmentation-prone" skin usually means

Many Indian skin tones make pigment easily. That is not a flaw. It is simply how melanin-rich skin protects itself. But it does mean small triggers can leave marks that stay for weeks or months.

Common signs that your skin is pigmentation-prone:

  • Old pimples leave brown or dark marks long after the bump has gone.
  • Your cheeks, forehead or upper lip look darker after a few days in strong sun.
  • Waxing, threading, scratching or a harsh scrub leaves a dark patch.
  • Your skin tone looks uneven around the mouth, eyes or jaw.

Heat, strong UV light, pollution and friction all play a part, and Indian summers bring plenty of all four.

Sunscreen is not optional

This is the part most people skip, and it is the reason many give up. UV light is one of the biggest triggers for dark patches. If you use a cream at night but stay unprotected in the day, the sun keeps making new pigment faster than any cream can help.

  • Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, every morning.
  • Use about two finger-lengths for the face and neck.
  • Reapply if you are outdoors, sweating, or sitting near a sunny window.
  • Wear it on cloudy and monsoon days too. UV passes through clouds.

A cap, dupatta or umbrella in the afternoon sun also makes a real difference.

A simple night routine for pigmentation-prone skin

  1. Cleanse gently. Wash off sunscreen and dust with a mild face wash. Do not scrub hard. Friction can make pigment worse.
  2. Pat dry, leaving the skin slightly damp.
  3. Apply a pea-sized amount of cream. Warm it between your fingertips and press it over the face and neck, avoiding the eye area.
  4. Start slowly. If kojic acid is new to you, use it every second night for two weeks, then move to nightly use if your skin is comfortable.
  5. Sunscreen the next morning. Every day, without exceptions.

How long it takes

Skin renews itself roughly every 4–6 weeks, so pigment fades slowly. Here is a realistic timeline with nightly use and daily sunscreen:

  • First 2 weeks: skin feels softer and more moisturised. Dark patches look about the same.
  • Weeks 4–6: some marks may start to look a little softer at the edges.
  • Weeks 8–12: skin tone can look more even, and dark patches less noticeable.

Everyone's skin is different. Older, deeper marks take longer, and some patches will not change much with any cosmetic cream. Take a photo in the same light every two weeks so you can judge honestly.

Mistakes that make dark patches look worse

  • Stacking several strong actives at once, such as kojic acid, strong acids and retinoids on the same night.
  • Applying lemon, baking soda or toothpaste to marks. These can irritate skin and leave more pigment.
  • Picking pimples or scratching dry patches.
  • Skipping sunscreen on "indoor" days.
  • Using unlabelled creams that promise very fast change. Read why in our avocado night cream side effects guide.

When to see a dermatologist

A cosmetic night cream is for everyday care. Please see a dermatologist if:

  • You have brown or grey-brown patches on both cheeks, the forehead, nose or upper lip that keep spreading. This can be melasma, which often needs medical care.
  • Patches appeared during pregnancy, after starting birth control pills, or after a hormonal change.
  • A dark spot is changing shape, size or colour, bleeds or itches.
  • You have acne that is painful or cystic and keeps leaving marks.
  • You are pregnant or breastfeeding and want to use kojic acid or other actives.

A dermatologist can tell you what kind of pigmentation you have, and that changes what will help.

Short answers

Can avocado night cream help with pigmentation? It can help dark patches look less noticeable over 8–12 weeks, when used with daily sunscreen.

Is kojic acid safe for Indian skin? Most people tolerate it well in cosmetic amounts. Patch test first and start every second night.

Why are my dark patches not changing? The most common reason is skipping sunscreen. Deep or hormonal patches such as melasma may also need a dermatologist.

Can I use it during pregnancy? Please check with your doctor first before using creams with kojic acid or other actives.

Should I use it in the morning too? Keep it for the night. In the morning, use a light moisturiser and sunscreen.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
