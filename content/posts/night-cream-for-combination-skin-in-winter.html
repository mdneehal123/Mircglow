Winter changes combination skin. Cheeks get drier and tighter, while the forehead, nose and chin can still feel oily. You do not need two creams. You need a small adjustment to how much you use and where you use it.

What changes in winter

Cold, dry air and indoor heating pull water out of the skin. The cheeks, which are already the drier zone, can feel tight or flaky. The T-zone often slows down but rarely goes fully dry. That is why one heavy cream all over can make the T-zone congested, while a light cream may not be enough for the cheeks.

The winter zone method

  1. Cleanse with lukewarm water. Hot water strips the skin and makes dryness worse.
  2. Apply your night cream to the cheeks first. Press in a pea-sized amount.
  3. Add a thin second layer to dry cheeks. Only if they still feel tight after a few minutes.
  4. Use the leftover on the T-zone. That is enough for the forehead, nose and chin.
  5. Keep lips and the sides of the nose in mind. A tiny extra dot helps if they flake.

Winter mistakes to avoid

  • Switching to a very thick cream for the whole face.
  • Hot showers and hot face washes.
  • Skipping sunscreen because the sun feels weaker.
  • Exfoliating more often than twice a week.

Short answers

Should combination skin use a richer cream in winter? Only on the dry areas. Keep the T-zone light.

Can I use two creams? You can, but most people do well with one cream and a second thin layer on the cheeks.

Do I still need sunscreen in winter? Yes, in the morning.

How much night cream should I use? A pea-sized amount for the whole face, a little more on dry cheeks.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
