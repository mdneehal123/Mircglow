Kojic acid vs alpha arbutin: both are spot-focused ingredients that slow tyrosinase, the enzyme skin uses to make pigment, so both help dark spots, old pimple marks and tanning patches look less noticeable over time. Kojic acid has the longer track record and is used in many Indian creams, but it can tingle on sensitive skin and is less stable in a jar; alpha arbutin is known for being gentler and more stable, which makes it a common pick for sensitive or easily irritated skin.

Neither one changes your natural skin colour, and neither works without daily sunscreen. Below is how to choose between them, whether to use them together, and how to tell which one your skin prefers.

How each ingredient works

Kojic acid is made by certain fungi during fermentation, for example of rice. In face creams it is typically used at about 1–2%. It limits how much new pigment the skin makes, so as skin renews, the newer layers come through looking more even.

Alpha arbutin is a stable form of arbutin, a compound found in plants such as bearberry. It is usually used at up to about 2% and works on the same enzyme in a gentle, steady way. Our alpha arbutin cream guide covers the basics and how to apply it.

Both work on pigment that is about to be made, not on marks that are already there. That is why results follow the skin's renewal cycle of roughly 4 to 6 weeks, and a more even look usually takes 8 to 12 weeks.

Which is stronger?

There is no simple winner. Both are used at cosmetic levels, both work slowly, and how well each suits you depends on your skin more than on the ingredient's reputation. Kojic acid is the older, more widely used choice; alpha arbutin is the one people often move to when kojic acid feels too much.

What matters more than "strength" is whether you can use the cream every night for three months without irritation, and whether you wear sunscreen every morning. A gentle ingredient used steadily will do more than a stronger one you keep stopping because it stings.

Who should pick which

Kojic acid may suit you if…

  • Your skin is normal, oily or combination and rarely reacts to new products.
  • You are dealing with tanning or sun spots from two-wheeler commutes or outdoor work.
  • You want an affordable everyday night cream that also moisturises.

Alpha arbutin may suit you if…

  • Your skin is sensitive, or kojic acid has tingled, itched or reddened before.
  • You want to use a spot-focused cream in the morning as well, under sunscreen.
  • Your skin is going through a dry phase, such as winter or long hours in AC, when stinging is more likely.

If you are not sure what you are dealing with, dark spots vs acne marks helps you tell the difference first.

Can you use kojic acid and alpha arbutin together?

They do not clash, and some routines include both. But you rarely need two separate spot products. Stacking them does not make spots fade faster, and it raises the chance of dryness or tingling.

  • Simplest: pick one cream with one spot-focused ingredient and use it every night.
  • If you own both: alternate nights rather than layering, after patch testing each one separately.
  • If you layer: wait until your skin has used each cream alone for two weeks without trouble, then try them together on alternate nights first.
  • Keep them apart from retinol and exfoliating acids. Use those on different nights, if at all.

Niacinamide pairs comfortably with either one. See niacinamide and kojic acid together.

Signs an ingredient is not suiting you

  • Tingling that lasts more than a few minutes, or that gets worse each night.
  • Redness, itching or small rough bumps where you applied the cream.
  • Dry, flaky patches that were not there before.

Stop the cream, use only a plain moisturiser and let skin settle for a few days. A small number of people react to kojic acid specifically; our guide to kojic acid side effects explains what to watch for. If swelling, blistering or a spreading rash appears, see a dermatologist.

Getting results with either

  1. Patch test behind the ear or on the jawline for 24 to 48 hours.
  2. Apply at night to clean, dry skin: a pea-sized amount over the whole face, not just the spots.
  3. Sunscreen every morning, SPF 30 or higher, reapplied every 2 to 3 hours outdoors.
  4. Take a photo in the same light every four weeks. Day-to-day changes are too small to notice in the mirror.
  5. Judge after 8 to 12 weeks. See how long dark spots can take to fade.

See a dermatologist for melasma, patches that change quickly, or before using either ingredient if you are pregnant or breastfeeding.

Short answers

Is kojic acid better than alpha arbutin? Neither is better. Kojic acid has the longer track record; alpha arbutin is usually gentler and more stable.

Which is better for sensitive skin? Alpha arbutin is the more common choice for sensitive skin. Patch test either way.

Can I use kojic acid and alpha arbutin together? Yes, they do not clash, but one spot ingredient per night is usually enough.

Why has my kojic acid cream turned darker? Kojic acid can darken with heat and light. Store it somewhere cool and shaded, and if the smell or texture has changed too, it may have gone off.

Do I need sunscreen with alpha arbutin? Yes. Without daily sunscreen, new pigment forms faster than any cream can keep up with.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
