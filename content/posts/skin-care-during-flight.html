Skin care during a flight is mostly about moisture: start with a clean, moisturised face, skip heavy makeup, sip water, and reapply a small amount of cream or balm every few hours on long flights. Air inside a plane cabin is much drier than air on the ground, so even oily skin can feel tight, and lips, hands and the area around the eyes dry out first.

Whether it is a 2-hour domestic hop from Bengaluru to Delhi or a long flight to the Gulf, the UK or the US, the same simple plan works. Below is what to do before, during and after flying, a hand-baggage kit and a routine for landing in a different climate.

Before you leave home

  1. Wash your face gently and apply moisturiser on slightly damp skin. For early-morning flights, this can be your whole routine.
  2. Apply sunscreen if you fly in the day, especially on a window seat. Daylight comes through the window for hours.
  3. Keep makeup light. A tinted moisturiser or nothing at all is easier on skin for a long flight than full foundation. You can do makeup after landing.
  4. Do not start a new product the night before. Travel is not the time to test a new acid or retinol. Patch test new creams at home first; our patch test guide shows how.

Your hand-baggage skin kit

Hand-baggage rules for liquids, gels and creams change, so check your airline and airport rules before you pack. Small jars and tubes are easiest.

  • Lip balm.
  • Small hand cream.
  • Sunscreen in a travel tube for day flights.
  • A few face wipes or a small face wash for after landing.
  • Hand sanitiser, used before you touch your face.

Leave face mists with alcohol and strong active serums at home for the flight. A mist that dries on the skin can leave it feeling tighter than before.

During the flight

  1. Drink water regularly. Ask for water each time the trolley comes, and do not rely only on tea, coffee or juice.
  2. Reapply lip balm every hour or two.
  3. On flights longer than about 4 hours, clean your hands, then press a thin layer of cream over cheeks, around the nose and on the neck. Do not rub hard over makeup.
  4. Keep hands off your face and wipe the tray and armrest if you plan to rest your face on your arms.
  5. Sleep with your face clean. If you nap on a long flight, a light layer of cream is better than sleeping in heavy makeup.
  6. Go easy on salty snacks if your eyes tend to puff up after flying.

Red-eye flights and jet lag

Night flights and time-zone changes mean less sleep, which often shows as a tired, dull look for a day or two. Keep the routine simple: water, a gentle wash, moisturiser and sunscreen in the morning, and an early night once you can. Our guide on skin care for lack of sleep has a realistic routine for these days. Nothing makes the tired look vanish in an hour; rest and moisture help most.

Frequent flyers and cabin crew-style routines

  • Keep one travel kit always packed so you never fly without lip balm and cream.
  • Use a simple, gentle cream on flight days and save strong actives for nights at home.
  • Wash pillow covers and face towels often if you travel weekly.

When to see a doctor

See a dermatologist or doctor if you get a rash, hives or swelling on a trip, skin that cracks and bleeds, or breakouts that are painful and keep coming back. If you have a skin condition such as eczema, ask your doctor what to carry for long flights.

Short answers

What should I apply on my face before a flight? A moisturiser on clean, slightly damp skin, sunscreen for day flights, and light or no makeup. Carry lip balm and a small cream for long flights.

Why does my skin get dry on a plane? Cabin air is very dry, so it pulls water from your skin. Moisturising before and during long flights and drinking water help.

Can I sleep with makeup on a flight? It is better not to. Wipe off heavy makeup and apply a thin layer of cream before you nap on a long flight.

Is face mist good on a plane? Plain water mist dries off quickly and can leave skin tighter. If you use one, press a little cream on top afterwards.

Can I carry face cream in hand baggage in India? Small jars and tubes are usually allowed, but liquid and cream rules can change. Check your airline and airport rules before packing.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
