Skin care for Amritsar weather means strong sun protection and a light moisturiser through the very hot, dry summer from April to June, fewer layers in the humid monsoon from July to September, and a rich night cream, lip balm and warm (not hot) baths in the cold, foggy winter from December to February. A gentle face wash and broad-spectrum sunscreen stay the same all year; your moisturiser and night cream change with the season.

Amritsar sits on the open Punjab plains, far from the sea, so it swings between extremes: summer afternoons that feel like an oven, a sticky monsoon, and some of the coldest, foggiest winter mornings in north India. Most skin trouble here comes from dry heat and dust in summer and from cold air, heaters and hot water in winter.

A simple daily routine for Amritsar skin

Morning

  1. Gentle face wash with lukewarm water. On cold winter mornings, a splash of water is enough if your skin feels dry.
  2. Moisturiser: a thin gel or lotion from April to October, a creamier one from November to February. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has only avocado and aloe vera, so it works well as a daytime moisturiser.
  3. Broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, about two finger-lengths for face and neck. Wear it in foggy winter too; UV still reaches your skin through haze.

Night

  1. Cleanse to remove sunscreen, sweat, dust and smoke.

Amritsar summer: dry heat, hot winds and dust

From April to June, afternoons get extremely hot and the air is dry and dusty. Skin tans fast, and many people wash their face again and again to feel cool, which leaves it tight and rough.

  • Stay in shade from late morning to late afternoon where you can, and cover with a cap, dupatta, umbrella or light full sleeves. Covering works better than any cream alone.
  • Reapply sunscreen after lunch if you are outdoors and after heavy sweating.
  • Splash plain water when you get home instead of using face wash many times a day; use face wash twice a day at most.
  • Keep a thin moisturiser on. Dry heat pulls water out of skin even when your face feels oily.

Monsoon in Amritsar

The rains usually arrive in July. Humidity rises sharply, sweat stays on skin, and pores clog more easily.

  • Use lighter textures and fewer layers: face wash, gel moisturiser and sunscreen are often enough in the morning.
  • Use half your usual night cream, or only on dry areas such as cheeks.
  • Change out of damp clothes soon after getting wet, and dry underarms, the neck and the gaps between your toes fully.

Amritsar winter: fog, cold air, heaters and hot baths

December and January bring dense fog, cold mornings and cold nights. Cold air holds little moisture, and long hot baths, heaters and blowers make dryness much worse.

  • Switch to a creamier moisturiser and a full pea-sized night cream from about late November.
  • Keep bath water warm, not very hot, and keep baths short. Very hot water strips the skin's natural oils.
  • Apply moisturiser and body lotion on slightly damp skin within a couple of minutes of your bath.
  • Use lip balm through the day and before bed; avoid licking dry lips.
  • If you sleep with a heater on, keep a bowl of water in the room and moisturise before bed. See room heater skin care.

Golden Temple visits, Wagah and Lohri nights

Many days in Amritsar mean long hours outdoors, often barefoot or in crowds.

  1. Golden Temple parikrama: the marble can get very hot on summer afternoons, so early morning or late evening visits are easier on your feet. Wash and dry your feet afterwards and moisturise your heels at night.
  2. Wagah border ceremony: you sit in open stands for a long time in the late-afternoon sun. Apply sunscreen before you leave, carry a cap and reapply on your nose, cheeks and the back of the neck.
  3. Lohri bonfire (13 January): sitting close to the fire for hours dries the face and lips. Sit a little back, moisturise before you go and use lip balm.
  4. Weddings in winter: long nights in the cold with heavy makeup. Remove makeup fully before sleep and use a richer night cream. Our winter wedding skincare guide has a full plan.

Hard water, dust and smoke

Water in many homes can be hard and leave skin feeling tight after a wash. A low-foam face wash and moisturiser straight afterwards help; see our hard water skincare guide. When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for sunburn with blisters, painful or cystic acne, a spreading itchy rash in skin folds or between the toes, cracked heels or hands that bleed, or patches of colour change that keep spreading. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before using creams with active ingredients.

Short answers

What is the best skin care routine for Amritsar weather? A gentle face wash, moisturiser and broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning, and a night cream at bedtime. Go lighter from April to October and richer from December to February.

Why is my skin so dry in Amritsar winter? Cold, foggy air, heaters and very hot baths pull moisture from skin. Use warm water, moisturise on damp skin, and use a full night cream and lip balm.

Do I need sunscreen on foggy winter days? Yes. Fog and haze cut brightness, not all UV, and you are often outdoors for long hours in winter.

How do I protect my skin at the Wagah border ceremony? Apply sunscreen before you go, wear a cap and reapply on your nose, cheeks and neck while you wait in the open stands.
