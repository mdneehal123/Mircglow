Why fake avocado night creams are a problem

A fake jar is not just a waste of money. Nobody knows who filled it, what is inside, how old it is or how it was stored. Some unregulated creams sold loose or through random pages may contain ingredients that should never be in an everyday face cream, which is why a quick, dramatic change on the skin is a warning sign, not a good sign.

How to check an avocado night cream original: 8 label checks

Hold the jar or box in good light and check each point.

  1. Sealed jar. The outer box or shrink wrap should be intact, and the jar should have an inner seal or foil under the lid. A jar that opens straight to the cream, with finger marks or a dented surface, may have been opened or refilled.
  2. Batch number. Look for "Batch No." or "B. No." printed clearly. It should match on the box and the jar if both carry it.
  3. Manufacturing and expiry dates. Both should be printed, not handwritten, and the expiry should be well in the future.
  4. Full ingredient list. Not just "avocado extract". A real cosmetic lists its ingredients, usually in order from most to least.
  5. Manufacturer name and address. A proper company name and a full address, not just a phone number or a social media handle.
  6. MRP printed. Indian rules require the maximum retail price on the pack. A sticker that covers or changes it is a reason to ask questions.
  7. Licence details. Cosmetics made in India normally show the manufacturing licence number on the label. A pack with no licence line at all is a red flag.
  8. Net weight. The weight in grams should be printed and should match what the listing says.

Check the cream itself: colour, smell and texture

Once the seal is broken, look and smell before you apply.

  • Colour. It should look even all through the jar, with no streaks, dark spots or a watery layer on top.
  • Smell. It should smell the same every time you buy it. A sour, rancid or chemical smell suggests an old or badly stored product.
  • Texture. Smooth and consistent. Grainy, split or very runny cream is a reason to stop and contact the seller.

Even with an original jar, do a patch test behind the ear or on the inner arm for 24 to 48 hours before using it on your face.

A very low price is one of the clearest signs. If a jar is selling for a fraction of the price on the brand's own site, ask why.

Where to buy an original avocado night cream

After you buy: use it the right way

An original cream still needs to be used well. Take a pea-sized amount at night on clean skin, and wear sunscreen every morning, because kojic acid makes daily sun protection important. Skin usually feels softer within the first few nights. A more even-looking tone takes 8 to 12 weeks of regular use with sunscreen, because skin renews roughly every 4 to 6 weeks.

Stop using any cream that stings, burns or causes a rash, and see a dermatologist if it does not settle, or if you are pregnant or have melasma or painful acne.
