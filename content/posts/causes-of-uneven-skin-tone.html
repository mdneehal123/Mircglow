The most common causes of uneven skin tone on face are sun exposure, marks left after pimples, hormones, friction, dryness and simple ageing. Each one makes some areas of skin produce more pigment, or reflect light differently, than the skin around them. The good news is that most of these can look more even with gentle care and daily sunscreen.

Finding your cause matters, because the fix is different for a tan line, an old acne mark or a hormonal patch. Below we go through each cause in plain words, what it usually looks like, and what tends to help.

1. Sun exposure: the biggest cause

UV rays tell skin to make melanin as protection. On Indian skin this happens quickly, so the parts of your face that catch the most sun, the forehead, cheekbones, nose and upper lip, become darker than the rest. Heat from cooking, long commutes and hot weather can deepen pigment too, not only direct sun.

Skincare for sun exposure marks

  • SPF 30 or higher every morning, including cloudy and monsoon days.
  • Reapply every 2 to 3 hours outdoors; add a cap, dupatta or umbrella.
  • Use a moisturiser with niacinamide, kojic acid or alpha arbutin at night.
  • Expect a more even look over 8 to 12 weeks, as long as sunscreen stays daily.

2. Marks left after pimples and irritation

When a pimple, bite, rash or burn settles, skin often leaves a flat brown mark called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Picking makes it darker and longer-lasting. 3. Hormones and melasma

Pregnancy, some contraceptive pills and other hormonal shifts can bring on larger, mirror-image brown patches on both cheeks, the forehead or upper lip. This is often melasma. It is triggered by sun and heat and tends to come back, so it needs a dermatologist rather than just a shop-bought cream.

4. Ageing, sun spots and age spots

Years of sun add up. From the late 20s and 30s, small, well-defined brown spots can appear on the cheeks, temples and backs of the hands. Skin also renews more slowly with age, roughly every 4 to 6 weeks in younger adults and more slowly later, so marks linger.

Face cream for sun spots and the appearance of age spots

  • Choose a cream with ingredients such as kojic acid (typically about 1–2%), alpha arbutin, niacinamide or vitamin C.
  • Use it at night over the whole face, not just on single spots.
  • Be realistic: creams may make age spots look softer, but they often stay. A dermatologist can explain in-clinic options.

5. Friction, rubbing and harsh products

Skin darkens where it is rubbed again and again: mask straps, spectacle frames, tight collars, rough towels and heavy scrubbing. Harsh home remedies such as lemon, baking soda or toothpaste also irritate skin and can cause new patches. Stop the rubbing first; no cream can keep up with daily friction.

6. Dryness, dead skin and dehydration

Sometimes skin is not darker at all, just dull. Dry, flaky skin scatters light, so it looks patchy and grey. AC rooms, hot showers and hard water make this worse. A regular moisturiser and gentle exfoliation once or twice a week make the surface look smoother and more even. Read avocado cream for dry skin for richer textures that help.

Other causes worth knowing

  • Pollution and smoke can add to dullness and uneven-looking skin over time.
  • Some medicines make skin more sun-sensitive. Read the leaflet or ask your doctor.
  • Thyroid, diabetes and other medical conditions can change skin colour in patches, for example dark, velvety skin on the neck. Please see a doctor if this appears.
  • Perfume on the neck plus sun can leave darker streaks on some people.

A simple routine for a more even-looking tone

  1. Morning: gentle cleanse, light moisturiser, SPF 30 or higher.
  2. Night: cleanse, then a spot-focused moisturiser over the whole face.
  3. Weekly: gentle exfoliation at most twice; no gritty scrubs.
  4. Always: one new product at a time, given at least 8 weeks.

Short answers

What is the most common cause of uneven skin tone on the face? Sun exposure, followed closely by marks left after pimples.

Can dehydration make skin tone look uneven? Yes. Dry, flaky skin scatters light and looks dull and patchy until it is moisturised.

Will a face cream for sun spots work without sunscreen? It will struggle. New pigment keeps forming with daily sun, so sunscreen is essential.

Do age spots go away with cream? They often stay, but steady care and sunscreen may help them look softer over months.

When should I worry about uneven skin tone? See a dermatologist for large symmetrical patches, sudden changes, velvety dark skin on the neck, or spots that change shape or colour.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
