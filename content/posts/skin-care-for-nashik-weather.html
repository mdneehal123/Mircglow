Skin care for Nashik weather means sun protection and a light gel moisturiser through the hot, dry months from March to May, a simpler routine with fewer layers in the monsoon from June to September, and a richer night cream and lip balm in the cool, dry winter from November to February. A gentle face wash and broad-spectrum sunscreen stay the same all year; only your moisturiser and night cream change with the season.

Nashik sits on a plateau in north Maharashtra, at the edge of the Western Ghats, so it is milder than Mumbai and less humid for most of the year. Its biggest skin challenges are strong sun, dry winter mornings that can get properly cold, and long days outdoors at temples, ghats and vineyards.

A simple daily routine for Nashik skin

Morning

  1. Gentle face wash with lukewarm water. On cold winter mornings, a splash of water alone is fine if your skin is dry.
  2. Moisturiser: a thin gel or lotion from March to October, something creamier from November to February. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has only avocado and aloe vera, which suits daytime use.
  3. Broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, about two finger-lengths for face and neck. The plateau sun is strong even when the air feels cool.

Night

  1. Cleanse to remove sunscreen, sweat and dust.
  2. Night cream: half a pea-sized amount in the humid months, a full pea-sized amount in winter. Our Avocado Korean Night Cream (25 g, ₹649) has avocado, aloe vera, vitamin B3 and kojic acid; if you use it, wear sunscreen every morning.
  3. Lip balm before bed from November to February.

Nashik summer: dry heat and strong sun

From March to May, days get hot and dry while nights stay a little cooler than in the plains. Skin can tan quickly and feel rough, especially on cheeks and the backs of hands.

  • Cover up with a cap, umbrella, dupatta or sleeves for midday errands; covering works better than any cream alone.
  • Reapply sunscreen after lunch if you are outdoors, and after heavy sweating.
  • Rinse sweat and dust off with plain water when you get home rather than washing your face many times a day.
  • Keep using a thin moisturiser. Dry heat still pulls water from skin.

Monsoon in Nashik and the ghats

The city gets a steady monsoon, while the ghat areas towards Igatpuri and Trimbakeshwar get far heavier rain and mist. Skin feels greasier and pores clog more easily.

  • Use lighter textures and fewer layers: face wash, gel moisturiser and sunscreen are often enough in the morning.
  • Use half your usual night cream, or only on dry areas.
  • On monsoon treks and waterfall trips, carry a small towel, dry socks and a sunscreen stick. Cloudy skies still let UV through.
  • Dry your feet and the gaps between your toes fully after walking in wet shoes.

Nashik winter: cold, dry mornings

Nashik winters are cooler than most of Maharashtra, and the area around it, such as Niphad, is known for some of the state's coldest mornings. Cold air holds little moisture, and hot baths make dryness worse.

  • Switch to a creamier moisturiser and a full pea-sized night cream from about November.
  • Apply moisturiser on slightly damp skin within a couple of minutes of your bath.
  • Keep bath water warm, not very hot, and keep showers short.
  • Use lip balm through the day, and a thick cream on hands and heels at night.
  • If you use a heater, keep a bowl of water in the room and moisturise before sleep; see room heater skin care.

Long days outdoors: temples, ghats, vineyards and fields

Pilgrim days at Ramkund, Panchavati or Trimbakeshwar, big festival crowds, vineyard visits and farm work around the district all mean many hours in open sun.

  1. Before you go: moisturiser, then sunscreen on face, neck, ears and backs of hands.
  2. Cover: a cap or scarf and light full sleeves.
  3. Every two hours: reapply sunscreen, or at least on the nose and cheeks.
  4. Barefoot temple walks: stone gets hot in the afternoon; go early where you can, wash feet afterwards and moisturise heels at night.
  5. After a river dip: rinse with clean water when possible, pat dry and moisturise.

Hard water and dust

Borewell water in many homes can be hard and leave skin feeling tight after a wash. A low-foam face wash and moisturiser straight afterwards help; see our hard water skincare guide. On dusty days, cleanse properly at night but skip harsh scrubs.

Nashik vs Pune and Mumbai

Pune has a similar plateau climate with a longer, damper monsoon, so our Pune weather guide is a close match. Mumbai is far more humid for most of the year; if you travel there often, see the Mumbai weather guide.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for sunburn with blisters, painful or cystic acne, a spreading itchy rash in skin folds or between the toes, cracked skin that bleeds, or patches of colour change that keep spreading. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before using creams with active ingredients.

Short answers

What is the best skin care routine for Nashik weather? A gentle face wash, moisturiser, broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning and a night cream at bedtime. Go lighter from March to October and richer from November to February.

Why is my skin so dry in Nashik winter? Cold, dry mornings and hot baths pull moisture from skin. Use warm water, moisturise on damp skin and use a full night cream and lip balm.

Do I need sunscreen in Nashik winter? Yes. The days are bright and the sun is strong on the plateau, even when it feels cool.

How do I care for skin on a Trimbakeshwar or Igatpuri monsoon trip? Wear sunscreen even under clouds, carry a towel and dry socks, and rinse and moisturise when you get home.
