Dry skin around the nose is usually caused by a cold, constant nose-blowing with rough tissues, and dry winter air, which together strip the thin skin at the sides of the nostrils. The simple fix is to blow gently with soft cloth or tissue, rinse with plain water instead of soap, and keep a thin layer of a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser or petroleum jelly on the area, especially at night.

In India this shows up most from November to February: colds and allergies, room heaters and smoggy air in the north, and hot baths with hard water. Below are the common causes, a step-by-step routine, and what to avoid putting on raw, flaky skin.

Step-by-step routine while you have a cold

  1. Blow gently, one nostril at a time. Use soft tissues or a soft, clean cotton hanky. Dab the area dry instead of wiping hard.
  2. Rinse with plain lukewarm water once or twice a day. Skip soap and face wash on the raw part around the nostrils until it settles.
  3. Pat dry and apply a barrier. A thin layer of plain petroleum jelly or a fragrance-free balm on the nostril edges protects the skin from repeated wiping.
  4. Moisturise the rest of the face on slightly damp skin with a plain, gentle cream.
  5. Repeat the barrier at night and after every few rounds of nose-blowing during the day.

Keep any menthol or eucalyptus balm you use for a blocked nose off the broken skin around the nostrils; it can sting. Follow the label for anything meant to go inside the nose, and ask a doctor or pharmacist if a cold lasts more than about 10 days or you have a high fever.

Oily skin but dry around the nose?

This is common. The nose itself can be oily while the creases beside it flake, especially with a cold. Do not stop moisturising your whole face; just use a light layer on oily zones and a little more on the dry creases. Our guide to oily skin in winter explains how to balance the two.

When to see a doctor

See a doctor or dermatologist if the skin cracks and bleeds, crusts with yellow or honey-coloured scabs, becomes very painful or swollen, keeps flaking with greasy scales for weeks, or if nosebleeds are frequent. These need proper medical care, not a cosmetic cream. Patch test any new product before using it on irritated skin; our patch test guide shows how.

Short answers

Why is the skin around my nose dry and peeling? Usually a mix of nose-blowing during a cold, dry winter air and harsh washing. Gentle wiping, plain water and a barrier of jelly or balm help it feel comfortable again.

Can I put petroleum jelly around my nose? Yes, a thin layer of plain petroleum jelly on the skin around the nostrils is a common, simple barrier. Keep it on the outside skin.

Can I use my night cream on dry skin around the nose? A plain moisturiser is fine. Skip creams with kojic acid, glutathione, vitamin C or retinol on that flaky area until it settles.

Should I scrub off the flakes? No. Scrubbing raw skin makes it worse. Let a moisturiser soften the flakes and they will come away on their own.

How long does it take to feel better? It depends on how raw the skin is and how long the cold lasts. If it is not getting better after your cold has passed, see a dermatologist.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
