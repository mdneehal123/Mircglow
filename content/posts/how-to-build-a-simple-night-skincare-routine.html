A night skincare routine does not need ten products. Four steps are enough for most people: cleanse, tone, treat and moisturise. Together they take about three minutes, and done every evening they leave skin softer, calmer and smoother-looking by morning.

Why the evening routine matters most

During the day your skin deals with sun, dust, sweat and sunscreen. At night none of that is in the way. A clean face and a good cream get seven or eight undisturbed hours to work. If you only have time for one proper routine a day, make it the evening one.

The 4-step night skincare routine

1. Cleanse

Wash off the day with a gentle face wash and lukewarm water. If you wore sunscreen or make-up, cleanse twice: an oil cleanser or balm first, then the face wash. Pat dry and leave the skin slightly damp.

2. Tone

An alcohol-free toner is optional, but it adds a light layer of hydration. Pat it in with your palms rather than rubbing with cotton.

3. Treat

This is the step for one targeted product, such as a serum or a treatment cream. For a dull or uneven-looking tone, a thin layer of a cream with glutathione, vitamin C or kojic acid works well here. Use one treatment at a time so you can tell how your skin responds.

4. Moisturise

Finish with a night cream to seal everything in. A pea-sized amount covers the face, with a little extra for the neck. Richer creams suit dry skin. Oily skin needs only a thin layer.

How long it takes

  • Cleanse: about one minute.
  • Tone: about thirty seconds.
  • Treat: about thirty seconds.
  • Moisturise: about one minute.

Do it twenty to thirty minutes before bed, so the cream settles before your face meets the pillow. The full technique is in how to apply night cream.

Adjust it to your skin type

  • Dry skin: a rich cream and a full pea-sized amount. See avocado cream for dry skin.
  • Oily skin: keep the toner light and use half a pea of cream. See night cream for oily skin.
  • Sensitive skin: skip the treatment step for the first two weeks and patch test anything new.

Mistakes that undo a good routine

  • Sleeping in sunscreen or make-up.
  • Starting several new products in the same week.
  • Using too much cream.
  • Skipping sunscreen the next morning, which matters most if your night cream contains kojic acid.
