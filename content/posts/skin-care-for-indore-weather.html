Skin care for Indore weather means changing your routine with three clear seasons: a hot, dry summer, a humid monsoon, and a cool, dry winter with cold nights. A gentle face wash, a moisturiser, broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning and a night cream that you keep light in the monsoon and use more generously from November to February suit most skin in Indore.

Indore sits on the Malwa plateau in central India, so it is neither coastal and sticky nor as smoggy as the northern plains. Its nights are pleasant for much of the year, but summer days are harsh and winter air is dry. This guide gives one simple routine and what to change in each season.

The pattern: protect from sun and dryness in summer, keep it light in the monsoon, and add moisture in winter. Right now, in November, most people in Indore can start using a little more night cream than they did in September.

A simple daily routine for Indore skin

Morning

  1. Gentle face wash with lukewarm water. In winter, cold-morning water that is too hot dries the face, so keep it warm, not steaming.
  2. Moisturiser: a thin layer in the monsoon, a little more in summer and winter when the air is dry. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has only avocado and aloe vera and no kojic acid, so it works as a plain moisturiser by day or night.
  3. Broad-spectrum sunscreen SPF 30 or higher, about two finger-lengths for face and neck, every day. Winter sun in central India is still bright.

Night

  1. Cleanse to remove sunscreen, sweat and road dust.
  2. Night cream: about half a pea-sized amount in the monsoon for oily skin, a full pea-sized amount in winter and on dry summer nights. Our Avocado Night Cream (30 g, ₹649) has avocado, aloe vera, vitamin B3 and kojic acid; if you use it, wear sunscreen every morning.
  3. Lip balm from November, when lips crack easily.

Indore summer: dry heat, dust and tan

April and May are the hardest months. Days are very hot and dry, the sun is strong from mid-morning, and warm winds carry dust. Unlike coastal cities, the air often feels dry rather than sticky, so skin can feel both sweaty and tight on the same day.

  • Cover up when you are out at midday: a cotton dupatta, scarf or cap, and full sleeves on a scooter.
  • Reapply sunscreen after lunch and after wiping sweat.
  • Rinse your face with cool water when you get home to wash off dust, then cleanse properly at night.
  • Use a light moisturiser even if you feel sweaty; dry heat pulls water out of the skin.
  • Drink enough water through the day.

If your face feels hot and tight after a long day out, our after-sun skincare guide helps that evening. Tan fades slowly over weeks with shade and daily sunscreen; no cream removes it overnight.

Two-wheelers and dusty roads

Many people in Indore get around by scooter or bike, which means sun on the face, wind and dust every day. Apply sunscreen before you leave, wear a helmet with a visor or a clean cotton face cover, and wash your face as soon as you reach home. Indore monsoon: humid, warm, oily

From late June to September the air turns humid and warm. Skin feels oilier, sweat sits longer, and damp clothes cause itchy folds.

  • Use a gel or very light moisturiser by day and half the usual night cream.
  • Dry your face, neck and skin folds fully after getting wet.
  • Change damp clothes and socks soon after reaching home.
  • Keep sunscreen on through cloudy days.

Indore winter: cold nights and dry air

From November to February, mornings and nights get cold, especially in December and January, and the air is dry. Skin that was oily in the monsoon can turn tight within a few weeks.

  • Moisturise within a few minutes of washing, while skin is still slightly damp.
  • Bathe in warm, not very hot, water and keep baths short.
  • Use a full pea-sized night cream on dry cheeks, around the nose and on the chin.
  • If you use a room heater, keep some distance from it and do not sit facing it for hours; see our room heater skin care guide.

Hard water, street food nights and Rangpanchami

Borewell water in many Indore homes can be hard, which may leave skin feeling tight and soap hard to rinse. A gentle face wash, a final splash of filtered or boiled-and-cooled water if your skin is sensitive, and moisturising soon after washing help; our hard water skin care guide explains more.

Late nights at Sarafa or Chappan Dukan are part of Indore life. If you come home late, still take off sunscreen or makeup with a gentle cleanse before sleeping. Indore's Rangpanchami celebrations mean a day of colours: apply moisturiser and sunscreen before you go out, wash colours off with lukewarm water and a gentle cleanser, and do not scrub hard or use lemon or bleach afterwards.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for painful or cystic acne, an itchy rash in skin folds that spreads, ring-shaped or scaly patches, cracked skin that bleeds in winter, or patches of colour change that keep growing. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before using creams with active ingredients.

Short answers

What is the best skin care routine for Indore weather? A gentle face wash, a moisturiser, broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning and a night cream. Keep it light in the monsoon and add more moisture from November to February.

Why does my skin feel dry in Indore in summer? Indore's summer heat is mostly dry, with dusty winds, so skin loses water even while you sweat. Use a light moisturiser and rinse off dust in the evening.

Do I need sunscreen in Indore in winter? Yes. Winter days in central India are often bright and sunny, so use SPF 30 or higher every morning.

How much night cream should I use in Indore? About half a pea-sized amount in the monsoon for oily skin and a full pea-sized amount in winter or for dry skin.

Is Indore weather more like Ahmedabad or Delhi? It has hot, dry summers like Ahmedabad but cooler nights, and a cold, dry winter without as much smog as Delhi. See our Ahmedabad weather guide to compare.
