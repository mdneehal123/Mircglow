Skin care for Kanpur weather means strong sun protection and a thin moisturiser through the hot, dry, dusty summer with loo winds from April to June, fewer layers in the humid monsoon from July to September, and a richer night cream, lip balm and warm (not hot) baths in the cold, foggy winter from December to February. A gentle face wash and broad-spectrum sunscreen stay the same all year; your moisturiser and night cream change with the season.

Kanpur sits on the flat Ganga plains of Uttar Pradesh, far from the sea, with busy roads and industry all around. Skin faces dry heat and dust in summer, sweat in the monsoon, and smoke followed by cold fog in winter.

A simple daily routine for Kanpur skin

Morning

  1. Gentle face wash with lukewarm water. On cold winter mornings, a splash of water is enough if your skin feels dry.
  2. Moisturiser: a thin gel or lotion from April to October, a creamier one from late November to February. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has only avocado and aloe vera, so it works well as a light daytime moisturiser.
  3. Broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, about two finger-lengths for face and neck. Wear it on foggy and hazy days too; UV still gets through.

Night

  1. Cleanse to remove sunscreen, sweat, dust and smoke.
  2. Night cream: half a pea-sized amount in summer and the monsoon, a full pea-sized amount in winter. Our Avocado Night Cream (30 g, ₹649) has avocado, aloe vera, vitamin B3 and kojic acid; if you use it, wear sunscreen every morning.
  3. Lip balm before bed from November to February, and a thick cream on heels.

Kanpur summer: loo, dust and long commutes

From late April to June, afternoons get extremely hot and the loo blows dry, dusty air. Many people travel by scooter, bike or e-rickshaw, so face, neck and hands get hours of sun and dust.

  • Cover first: a cap or helmet, a cotton dupatta or scarf, light full sleeves and sunglasses. Covering works better than any cream alone.
  • Reapply sunscreen after lunch if you are outdoors, and after heavy sweating.
  • When you get home, splash plain water instead of using face wash many times a day; use face wash twice a day at most.
  • Keep a thin moisturiser on. Dry heat pulls water out of skin even when your face feels oily.

Riding daily? Read skincare for bike riders. For hot, tight skin after a long day out, see the after-sun skincare guide. Tan fades slowly over weeks with shade and sunscreen; no cream removes it overnight.

Monsoon in Kanpur

The rains usually arrive in late June or early July. Humidity rises, sweat stays on skin, and lanes can stay waterlogged after heavy showers.

  • Use lighter textures and fewer layers: face wash, gel moisturiser and sunscreen are often enough in the morning.
  • Use half your usual night cream, or only on dry areas such as cheeks.
  • If you walk through dirty rainwater, wash your feet and legs with soap and clean water soon after, and dry the gaps between your toes fully.

October and November: smoky, hazy air

After the monsoon, the weather turns pleasant, but many weeks are hazy with smoke and dust, especially around Diwali. Skin often looks dull and feels grimy by evening.

  • Cleanse properly every night so sunscreen, dust and soot do not sit on skin while you sleep.

Kanpur winter: fog, cold waves, heaters and hot water

December and January bring dense fog, cold mornings and some cold-wave days. Cold air holds little moisture, and long hot baths, heaters and blowers make dryness much worse.

  • Switch to a creamier moisturiser and a full pea-sized night cream from about late November.
  • Keep bath water warm, not very hot, and keep baths short. Very hot water strips the skin's natural oils.
  • Apply moisturiser and body lotion on slightly damp skin within a couple of minutes of your bath.
  • If you sleep with a heater or sit near an angithi, sit a little back, keep a bowl of water in the room and moisturise before bed. See room heater skin care.

Hard water, Ganga ghats and winter weddings

  1. Hard water: borewell and supply water in many homes can be hard and leave skin tight after washing. Use a low-foam face wash and apply moisturiser straight afterwards; our hard water skincare guide explains more.
  2. Ghat visits and Bithoor trips: after a holy dip or a long morning by the river, rinse with clean water when you can, dry off fully and moisturise. Sunscreen before you go matters most from March to October.
  3. Winter weddings: long nights in the cold with heavy makeup. Remove makeup fully before sleep and use a richer night cream; see our winter wedding skincare guide.

Kanpur vs Lucknow and Delhi

Lucknow is close by and has almost the same year, with loo summers, a humid monsoon and foggy winters, so our Lucknow weather guide is a close match. Delhi has a similar pattern with an even smokier winter; if you travel there often, read the Delhi weather guide.

When to see a dermatologist

See a dermatologist for sunburn with blisters, painful or cystic acne, a spreading itchy rash in skin folds or between the toes, cracked heels or hands that bleed, or patches of colour change that keep spreading. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before using creams with active ingredients.

Short answers

What is the best skin care routine for Kanpur weather? A gentle face wash, moisturiser and broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning, and a night cream at bedtime. Go lighter from April to October and richer from December to February.

How do I protect my skin from the loo in Kanpur? Cover your face, neck and arms, stay in shade in the hottest hours, wear sunscreen and reapply after lunch, and keep a thin moisturiser on.

Why is my skin so dry in Kanpur winter? Cold, foggy air, heaters and very hot baths pull moisture from skin. Use warm water, moisturise on damp skin, and use a full night cream and lip balm.
