Skin care for cricket players comes down to three things: plenty of water-resistant sunscreen before you go out, reapplying it at every drinks or innings break, and a gentle wash and moisturiser once the match is over. Hours in the open sun, sweat under caps and helmets, and dust from the pitch are what strain skin most, so a short routine around these moments matters more than any special product.

This guide is for anyone who plays regularly in India: weekend league matches, net practice, box cricket or gully cricket on the street. It covers what to carry in your kit bag, what to do before, during and after a game, and how to look after sweat bumps, burnt lips and tired, dry skin.

Why cricket is hard on skin

  • Long hours in the sun. A match or practice session can run from morning into the afternoon, through the strongest sun of the day.
  • Light reflected from the ground. Dry grounds, matting pitches and concrete in gully cricket bounce extra light back onto the face.
  • Sweat under caps, helmets and pads. Trapped sweat and friction can lead to small bumps on the forehead, jawline and back.
  • Dust and wind. Dusty grounds leave a gritty layer on skin, and dry winter matches leave lips and cheeks chapped.
  • Spots that get missed. Ears, the back of the neck, the nose and forearms take a lot of sun and are easy to forget.

Before you play: the 15-minute setup

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash and pat dry.
  2. A thin moisturiser if your skin is dry, especially in winter. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has only avocado and aloe vera, with no kojic acid, so it sits well under sunscreen.
  3. Broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher. Use about two finger-lengths for the face and neck, and more for arms if they are bare. Apply about 15 minutes before you step out.
  4. Do not miss ears, the back of the neck, the nose, the hairline and the backs of your hands.
  5. Lip balm with SPF, because lips burn and crack easily on long days.
  6. Cap or wide-brim hat when fielding, and sunglasses where you can.

During the match: when to reapply

Sweat and wiping your face with a towel or sleeve remove sunscreen, so one coat in the morning does not last the day. Water-resistant sunscreen still needs reapplying.

MomentWhat to do
Every drinks break (or about every 2 hours)Blot sweat with a clean towel, then reapply sunscreen to face, ears and neck
Innings break or lunchRinse face with water if dusty, pat dry, full sunscreen layer again, lip balm
After batting with a helmetWipe the forehead and jawline, let skin air for a minute, reapply sunscreen
Fielding in the deepWear a cap, reapply on ears and neck, which face the sun all session
Box or gully cricket at nightNo sunscreen needed under lights; wash off dust and sweat afterwards

Drink water through the game as well. It keeps you feeling better in the heat, though it will not replace sunscreen.

Sweat bumps from helmets and caps

Helmet pads, caps and headbands trap sweat against the skin. This can lead to small red bumps or clogged pores on the forehead, hairline and chin.

  • Wash or air out helmet liners, caps and headbands often; a sweaty cap reused all week is a common cause.
  • Take the helmet off when you are not batting and let the skin breathe.
  • Wash your face as soon as you can after the match. Do not scrub hard.
  • Do not squeeze bumps; this can leave dark marks that last for weeks.

After the match: a simple routine

  1. Shower or wash within an hour to remove sweat, dust and sunscreen. Use lukewarm water, not very hot water.
  2. Gentle face wash, once. Double scrubbing irritates sun-tired skin.
  3. Cool down hot, tight skin with a cool, damp cloth or plain aloe vera gel.
  4. Moisturise. On match days, a plain soothing moisturiser is kinder than strong actives.

Tan and uneven tone from cricket

Regular players often notice tan lines on the forearms, neck and around the cap line. Tan from sun exposure usually fades slowly when you protect the skin well; no cream makes it disappear quickly. Young players

Children who play cricket need sunscreen, caps and water breaks too. Use a sunscreen made for children and ask a paediatrician for babies and toddlers.

When to see a doctor or dermatologist

See a doctor for sunburn with blisters, fever or chills, or if you feel dizzy or sick in the heat. See a dermatologist for painful or spreading acne, or a mole that changes in size, shape or colour.

Short answers

Which sunscreen is good for cricket? A broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, applied generously and reapplied about every two hours or at each break.

Why do cricketers use zinc on their face? Zinc oxide is a mineral sunscreen filter. Thick zinc creams sit on the nose, lips and cheeks, which take the most sun.

How do I avoid tan while playing cricket? You cannot fully avoid it, but sunscreen reapplied through the day, a cap and covering forearms reduce it a lot.

Can I use night cream after a cricket match? Yes, if your skin feels normal. If it is red, burnt or stinging, use a plain moisturiser that night instead.

What should I do for pimples from wearing a helmet? Wash helmet liners and caps often, wash your face soon after playing, and do not squeeze. See a dermatologist if they become painful or widespread.
