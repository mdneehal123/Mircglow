The best night moisturiser for your face is the one whose texture matches your skin: a light gel-cream for oily skin, a medium cream for combination skin and a rich, cushioned cream for dry skin. Use a pea-sized amount on clean, slightly damp skin as the last step of your night routine, and wash it off in the morning before sunscreen.

A night moisturiser and a night cream do the same basic job. Both put back the water and oil that washing removes, so skin feels soft instead of tight when you wake up. The difference is mostly texture and extras, which is why picking by skin type matters more than picking by brand.

What makes a good night moisturiser

  • It feels comfortable after ten minutes. Not greasy on the nose, not tight on the cheeks. If it still feels sticky after ten minutes, use less or pick a lighter texture.
  • It has ingredients that hold water. Aloe vera and hyaluronic acid draw water in; oils and butters keep it from escaping.
  • It has no sunscreen in it. Sunscreen is for the day. At night it only adds weight.
  • It suits your budget for the long run. A moisturiser works when you use it every night, so a jar you can keep buying beats an expensive one you save for special days.

Night moisturiser or night cream: do you need both?

No. Pick one. A night cream is simply a moisturiser made for the evening, often a little richer, and sometimes with an extra ingredient such as niacinamide, kojic acid or glutathione for a brighter, more even look. If your night moisturiser already does what you want, you do not need a separate night cream on top. The full comparison is in night cream vs moisturiser.

How to use a night moisturiser

  1. Wash your face with a gentle cleanser. Remove make-up and sunscreen first.
  2. Pat dry, but leave the skin slightly damp.
  3. If you use a serum, apply it now and wait a minute.
  4. Dot a pea-sized amount of moisturiser on the forehead, cheeks, nose and chin, then spread it upward and outward.
  5. Apply it 20 to 30 minutes before bed so less ends up on the pillow.
  6. In the morning, wash your face and use sunscreen.

Night moisturiser for oily skin

Oily skin still needs a night moisturiser. Washing removes water as well as oil, and skin left dry can look shinier by morning. Use half a pea, keep the T-zone very thin, and skip it on the nose in the most humid months if you feel comfortable. More in best night cream for oily skin.

Night moisturiser for combination skin

Use one medium cream with the zone method: apply to the cheeks first, then use only what is left on your fingertips for the forehead, nose and chin. More in night cream for combination skin.

Night moisturiser for dry skin

Dry skin does best with a rich cream on slightly damp skin, every night. In winter, use a little more, avoid very hot water on the face and run a fan instead of a heater close to your bed if you can. More in best night cream for dry skin.

Short answers

Which is the best night moisturiser for face? The one whose texture suits your skin type: light for oily, medium for combination, rich for dry. Use it every night on slightly damp skin.

Can I use my day moisturiser at night? Yes, if it has no sunscreen in it. You may want a slightly richer texture at night in winter.

Should I apply moisturiser after serum at night? Yes. Serum first, wait a minute, then moisturiser on top to hold the serum's water in.

How much night moisturiser should I use? About a pea-sized amount for the face. Using more mostly ends up on the pillow.

Does a night moisturiser change skin colour? No. A moisturiser helps skin look softer, smoother and more even, but it does not change your natural skin colour.
