Skin care for a long car drive is mostly about two things: sunscreen on the side of your face that sits next to the window, and a little moisturiser to cope with hours of car AC blowing on your face. Add lip balm, a water bottle and a gentle wipe-down at rest stops, and your skin will handle a 6 to 10 hour road trip far better.

In India the driver sits on the right, so on a long highway drive the right cheek, right arm and right side of the neck get far more sun than the left. Passengers by the window have the same problem on their side. Below is what a drive does to skin, a small car kit, and a simple routine for before, during and after the trip.

Sun through car glass: why the window side tans

Car windows do not protect your skin completely. The front windscreen is usually laminated glass, which blocks more ultraviolet light, but side windows are often plain toughened glass that still lets a good share of UVA rays through. UVA is the part of sunlight linked with tanning and with skin looking older over the years, and it is there even when the car feels cool inside.

Dark sun-control films are restricted on Indian cars, so do not count on tint either. The simple fix is sunscreen. Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or more on your face, ears, neck and the backs of your hands, about 15 minutes before you set out. On a drive longer than two or three hours in daylight, reapply it at a rest stop, especially on the window side. If you drive daily for work, a cotton arm sleeve or a light full-sleeve shirt helps the arm more than any cream.

If you already have a driving tan, our guide on skincare after sun exposure and the one on night cream for tanned skin explain what to expect. Tan fades slowly with sun protection; there is no overnight fix, so be wary of any cream that promises one.

Your car skin kit

Keep one small pouch in the glove box or the door pocket. In summer, do not leave creams and sunscreen in a parked car in direct sun for hours, because heat can spoil them.

  • Sunscreen (SPF 30 or more, broad spectrum) in a small tube or stick.
  • A small jar of moisturiser. Our Avocado Glow Cream 15 g (₹399) has avocado and aloe vera only, no kojic acid, so it is easy to use in the day and travels well.
  • Lip balm, because AC dries lips fast.
  • Plain, mild face wipes or a clean cotton hanky and a bottle of water to rinse with.
  • Hand sanitiser, used before you touch your face after fuel stations and dhabas.
  • A cap, sunglasses and a cotton scarf or dupatta for stops in the sun.

Step-by-step routine for a road trip

Before you leave

  1. Wash your face with a gentle face wash.
  2. Apply a thin layer of moisturiser on slightly damp skin. Oily skin needs only a pea-sized amount.
  3. Apply sunscreen as the last step on face, ears, neck and hands. Two finger-lengths is a fair amount for the face and neck.
  4. Keep makeup light. Foundation plus sweat and dust for 8 hours is hard on skin.

During the drive

  1. Point the AC vents at your chest or the floor, not straight at your face.
  2. At every stop (every 2 to 3 hours is good for the driver anyway), clean your hands, then wipe or rinse your face gently and pat it dry.
  3. Press a little cream on dry areas such as cheeks and around the nose.
  4. Reapply sunscreen on daylight drives, mainly on the window side.
  5. Sip water regularly, not only tea and cold drinks, and use lip balm.

After you arrive

  1. Remove sunscreen and the day's dust with a gentle cleanser. If you wore makeup, see how to remove makeup before bed.
  2. Apply your usual night cream. A long drive is not the night to try a new active ingredient.
  3. If your skin feels hot or tight from the sun, skip scrubs and strong actives for a night and keep it simple.

Car AC, heaters and dry skin

AC pulls moisture out of the air, and a vent blowing on the same cheek for hours dries it even more, which is why one cheek can feel tighter after a trip. In winter the heater does the same, so use a slightly richer cream and lip balm; heading to the hills, see our hill station skin care guide. For daily AC dryness at work, our AC dry skin routine has more detail.

At night, a nourishing cream helps the skin feel soft again. You can browse our night creams; if you use one with kojic acid, such as Avocado Night Cream, keep it for night only and wear sunscreen the next morning, which you will need on the road anyway.

When to see a doctor

See a dermatologist if you get a painful sunburn with blisters, a rash after sun exposure, dark patches that keep spreading, or a mole or spot that changes. Patch test any new cream at home a few days before a trip; our patch test guide shows how.

Short answers

Do I need sunscreen inside a car? Yes, on daylight drives. Side windows let a good share of UVA through, so the window side of your face, neck and arm can tan over time.

Why is my right arm darker than my left? In India the driver sits on the right, so the right arm and right side of the face get more sun through the window. Sunscreen and a sleeve help.

Does car AC dry out the skin? Yes. Point the vents away from your face, use a moisturiser and lip balm, and drink water through the drive.

Can I keep creams in the car? Keep them in a pouch, but do not leave them in a hot parked car for hours; heat can spoil creams and sunscreen.

What should I do on the night after a road trip? Cleanse gently, apply your usual night cream, and skip scrubs or new actives that night.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
