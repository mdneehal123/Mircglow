A niacinamide night cream is simply a moisturiser with vitamin B3 in it, and it is one of the easiest active ingredients to use at night. Niacinamide is gentle, gets along with most other ingredients, and supports smoother, more even-looking skin without the irritation risk of stronger actives.

Here is what vitamin B3 does, why night is a good time for it, and how to choose and use one for your skin type.

Vitamin B3 and niacinamide: the same thing?

Niacinamide, also called nicotinamide, is a form of vitamin B3. A label may say either name. It is different from niacin, another form of vitamin B3 that is known for causing flushing when taken as a supplement. In skincare, niacinamide is the form you will see, and it is known for being well tolerated.

What niacinamide does for skin

  • Supports the skin barrier. It helps skin hold on to moisture, so it feels softer and less tight.
  • Helps oily skin look less shiny. Pores can look less obvious too, which is welcome in humid weather.
  • Supports a more even-looking tone. Over time it can reduce the look of dullness and old marks.
  • Helps skin look calmer. Many people find it eases the look of redness.

Most creams contain roughly 2 to 5 percent niacinamide, while some serums go up to 10 percent. Higher is not always better. Some people notice tingling or flushing at higher strengths, and in a cream you spread over your whole face every night, a moderate amount makes sense.

Why a niacinamide night cream makes sense

Night gives the cream several hours on your skin with no sweat, sun, makeup or dust to deal with. It does not need to sit neatly under sunscreen or foundation, which matters if a niacinamide serum has ever pilled under your makeup.

Night is also when most brightening ingredients, such as kojic acid and glutathione, are usually applied. Niacinamide sits comfortably alongside them, so one cream can do several jobs without adding steps.

What niacinamide pairs well with

  • Kojic acid: a gentle team for a more even-looking tone. Many creams combine them.
  • Hyaluronic acid: adds extra hydration, useful in AC rooms and winter.
  • Vitamin C: the old warning against mixing them does not match how modern products are made. If you feel flushing, use vitamin C in the morning and niacinamide at night.
  • Retinol: the two are often used in the same routine. Introduce retinol slowly and on its own first.
  • Exfoliating acids: fine for many people, but use them on separate nights if your skin is sensitive.

How to use a niacinamide night cream

  1. Take off makeup and sunscreen, then wash with a gentle face wash.
  2. Pat dry. Add a hydrating serum first if you use one.
  3. Take a pea-sized amount and dot it on the forehead, cheeks, nose and chin.
  4. Smooth it outward, then down onto the neck.
  5. If the cream also contains kojic acid, use it every second night for the first two weeks, then nightly.
  6. Wear sunscreen the next morning, SPF 30 or higher.

Skin renews itself roughly every four to six weeks. Expect skin to feel softer and more comfortable early on, and a more even look after 8 to 12 weeks of steady use with daily sunscreen. Our guide on how to apply night cream has more on technique.

Possible side effects

Niacinamide is usually very well tolerated. Occasionally it causes redness, tingling or itching, more often at high strengths or on already irritated skin. Remember that a night cream contains other ingredients too, so a reaction may come from something else in the jar.

  • Patch test behind the ear or on the jawline and wait 24 hours.
  • Stop if you get a rash, burning or swelling.
  • See a dermatologist if a reaction does not settle, or if you have a skin condition such as rosacea or eczema.
  • If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, check with your doctor, especially if the cream also contains kojic acid.

Short answers

Can I use a niacinamide night cream every night? Yes, for most people. Start slowly if it also contains kojic acid.

Is niacinamide good for oily skin? Yes. It helps oily skin look less shiny and suits a light layer in summer.

Can I use niacinamide with kojic acid? Yes. They are a gentle pair and are often found in the same cream.

Do I still need sunscreen? Yes, every morning, especially if your night cream contains brightening ingredients.

Is niacinamide the same as niacin? No. Both are forms of vitamin B3, but niacinamide is the gentle form used in skincare.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
