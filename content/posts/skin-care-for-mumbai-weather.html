Skin care for Mumbai weather is about keeping things light: a thin, non-greasy moisturiser, a broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning and a proper cleanse at night to remove sweat, dust and sunscreen. Mumbai is humid for most of the year and gets a very heavy monsoon from about June to September, so thick creams and many layers usually feel sticky and can leave skin congested.

Then there is the famous October heat after the rains, a short, mild winter, long commutes in crowded trains and traffic, salty sea breeze and cold AC offices. Here is how to keep one simple routine and adjust it through the Mumbai year.

Sunscreen in Mumbai: daily, including monsoon days

Mumbai sun is strong from mid-morning, and the glare off the sea and wet roads adds to it. Grey monsoon skies still let a lot of UV through.

  • Use a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher as the last morning step, about two finger-lengths for face and neck.
  • Gel or fluid sunscreens usually feel less heavy in humidity than thick creams.
  • Reapply before heading out again after lunch, and after wiping a sweaty face.
  • Evening walks on the sea face still count if the sun is out; a cap helps.

Monsoon in Mumbai: June to September

Mumbai rain is heavy and constant for weeks, and getting soaked on the way to the station is normal. Damp skin and damp clothes together cause most rainy-season trouble.

  • Carry a small towel or tissues. Pat your face dry after getting wet, then reapply a little moisturiser if your skin feels tight.
  • Change out of wet clothes and socks as soon as you can. Keep the neck, underarms and feet dry.
  • If you waded through flood water, wash your feet and legs with soap and clean water as soon as possible, and see a doctor for any cut or wound.
  • Pause new actives and strong scrubs if your skin feels irritated.

October heat and oily skin

When the rains stop, Mumbai often gets a hot, sticky few weeks with strong sun. Many people feel their skin is "very oily", but it is often oily and dehydrated at the same time: shiny on top, tight after washing.

  • Wash your face twice a day, plus once after a very sweaty commute if needed. More than that usually dries skin out and makes shine worse.
  • Blot with tissue paper during the day instead of washing again.
  • Change pillow covers two or three times a week in hot months.
  • Keep hair oil off the forehead and hairline overnight.

Local trains, traffic and AC offices

A typical Mumbai day can mean a hot platform, a packed train or a long ride in traffic, then hours in a cold office. That mix leaves the T-zone shiny and cheeks tight. Sea air and the short winter

Salty sea breeze and dust can leave skin feeling rough on windy days, especially near the coast. A gentle evening cleanse and a thin moisturiser are enough; there is no need for strong exfoliants. Short answers

Which moisturiser is best for Mumbai weather? A light gel or thin cream that absorbs quickly suits Mumbai's humid weather best; heavy, oily creams tend to feel sticky and may clog pores.

Do I need sunscreen in Mumbai during the monsoon? Yes. Clouds let much of the UV through, so apply a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every morning, even on rainy days.

Can I use night cream in Mumbai's humidity? Yes, in a thin layer on clean skin. Oily skin can use half a pea-sized amount and keep the T-zone light.

Why is my skin oily in October in Mumbai? The October heat brings strong sun and humidity together, so skin sweats and shines more. Gentle cleansing, light layers and blotting usually help.

Does Mumbai have a winter skin season? Only a short, mild one from about November to February. Most people just need a little extra cream on tight areas, mainly because of AC.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
