Glutathione vs niacinamide is not really a fight. Niacinamide is the better-known, everyday ingredient for oil balance, the look of pores and a stronger-feeling skin barrier, while glutathione is an antioxidant used for an all-over radiant, fresher look. They do different jobs, and they sit together happily in one cream or one routine.

So the useful question is which one your skin needs more right now. This guide compares them simply, shows who each suits, and explains how to use them together without overloading your skin.

What each ingredient is

Niacinamide

Niacinamide is a form of vitamin B3. It is stable, water-soluble and usually gentle, which is why it turns up in so many moisturisers and serums. Creams often use around 2–5%; some serums go higher. It is chosen to help skin look less shiny, help pores look smaller, support the skin barrier and make tone look more even over time. Our guide to vitamin B3 in night cream explains it in more depth.

Glutathione

Glutathione is made of three amino acids and is produced naturally inside the body. In a face cream, it is there as an antioxidant for a brighter-looking, more radiant finish rather than for any single spot. Research on glutathione applied to the skin is newer and smaller than for niacinamide, so it is fair to keep expectations modest. You can read more in our glutathione cream benefits guide.

Which is better for oily skin and pores?

For oily skin, niacinamide is usually the more useful pick. It helps skin look less greasy through the day and helps pores look less obvious. It cannot change your actual pore size, which is largely set by genes and age, but a steady routine can make a visible difference to shine. Our guide to niacinamide for pores and oily skin covers this in detail.

Glutathione does not do much for oil. If shine is your main complaint, start with niacinamide in a light moisturiser and a gentle face wash.

Which is better for dull skin and dark spots?

For dullness, glutathione is the ingredient people usually reach for, because its job is an overall glow. Niacinamide also helps tone look more even over time, so it is not out of the picture.

For individual dark spots and acne marks, neither ingredient works fast on its own. Spot-focused ingredients such as kojic acid or alpha arbutin, plus daily sunscreen, usually matter more. See our list of ingredients for dark spots for the full picture.

Which is better for sensitive or dry skin?

Both are generally well tolerated. Niacinamide has the edge for dry or easily upset skin because it supports the barrier, the outer layer that holds water in. If you are very sensitive, start with a cream that has a lower strength of niacinamide rather than a high-strength serum, and patch test first.

Glutathione rarely stings, but a cream containing it may also contain other actives such as kojic acid or vitamin C, which can bother sensitive skin. Always read the full ingredient list, not just the front of the box.

Can you use glutathione and niacinamide together?

Yes. There is no known conflict between them, and many creams contain both. Using them together gives you niacinamide's barrier and oil support with glutathione's radiant-look role.

  1. Keep it simple. One cream with both is easier than layering two serums.
  2. Night: cleanse, pat dry, apply a pea-sized amount of your cream to the face and a little to the neck.
  3. Morning: a gentle cleanse, a light moisturiser, then sunscreen as the last step.
  4. Add slowly. If you also use other actives, bring in one new product at a time. Our guide on how to introduce active ingredients shows a safe pace.

How long before you see a difference?

Skin renews roughly every 4–6 weeks. With niacinamide, many people notice less shine within a few weeks. For a more even, brighter look from either ingredient, give it 8–12 weeks of steady use with sunscreen every morning. Changing creams every two weeks makes it hard to judge anything.

Neither ingredient changes your natural skin colour. The honest aim is skin that looks fresher, smoother and more even.

Short answers

Is glutathione better than niacinamide? Not overall. Niacinamide is better studied and more versatile; glutathione is mainly for an all-over radiant look.

Can I use glutathione and niacinamide together? Yes. They have no known conflict and often come in the same cream.

Which is better for oily skin? Niacinamide, because it helps skin look less shiny and pores look smaller.

Which is better for dull skin? Glutathione is the usual pick for dullness, and niacinamide helps tone look more even over time.

Do I need sunscreen with these ingredients? Yes. Daily sunscreen is what protects any brighter, more even-looking result.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
