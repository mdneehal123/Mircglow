Kojic acid vs glutathione: if you have been shopping for a cream to brighten dull or uneven-looking skin, you have seen both names on nearly every label. They often sit in the same jar, which makes it even harder to tell what each one is for.

They are different things, and they do different jobs. Here is how to tell them apart and decide which you need.

Kojic acid, briefly

Kojic acid is made by certain fungi during fermentation. It is the same process that gives us sake and soy sauce. In skincare it is the ingredient brands reach for when the goal is the look of dark spots, tan and patchy tone. Creams usually contain a small amount, around one to two percent. Glutathione, briefly

Glutathione is a small antioxidant molecule your body makes for itself. In a cream, it is there for overall radiance: skin that looks fresh and bright across the whole face. More on that in glutathione cream benefits.

Kojic acid vs glutathione: the 5 differences that matter

1. Where they come from

Kojic acid is a product of fermentation. Glutathione is something already present in your body.

2. What they are good at

Kojic acid is the specialist. You choose it for particular spots and patches. Glutathione is the generalist, chosen for an all-over glow.

3. How gentle they are

Glutathione is usually the kinder of the two. Kojic acid is more likely to irritate sensitive skin in the early weeks, so it needs a slower start.

4. How well studied they are

Kojic acid has been used in cosmetics for decades. Research on glutathione applied to the skin is newer and limited to small studies.

5. What they ask of you

Both go on at night. Both need sunscreen in the morning. Kojic acid just wants a little more care: every second night to begin with, and an eye on how your skin responds.

So which one?

  • Kojic acid if your concern is the look of dark spots, old pimple marks or tan lines.
  • Glutathione if your skin simply looks dull and tired.
  • Both if you want radiance and a more even-looking tone together.

Can you use them together?

Yes, and many creams do it for you. Our Glutathione Cream has glutathione, vitamin C and kojic acid in a single jar, so there is nothing to layer.

If you have them in two separate products, put the lighter one on first and the richer one last. With sensitive skin, use them on alternate nights.

Using them safely

  1. Patch test behind the ear and wait a day.
  2. Every second night for the first two weeks.
  3. A pea-sized amount, on clean skin.
  4. Sunscreen every morning.
  5. Stop if it burns or you get a rash.

How long before you see anything?

Neither is quick. Softer, better-hydrated skin in the first week, and a more even look after eight to twelve weeks of nightly use with sunscreen. See how long glutathione cream takes to work.

Short answers

Which is stronger? Kojic acid is more targeted. Glutathione is gentler.

Which for sensitive skin? Glutathione is usually the easier start. Patch test either way.

Do I need both? No. One good cream, used every night with sunscreen, does most of the work.

Are they safe? Both are widely used in cosmetics. This is general information and not medical advice, so see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
