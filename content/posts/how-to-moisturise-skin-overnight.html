To moisturise skin overnight, wash gently, apply a hydrating layer to slightly damp skin, then seal it in with a night cream so water does not escape while you sleep. That simple order, plus a humidifier-friendly bedroom and a little extra care in AC rooms, is how to moisturise skin overnight and wake up with softer, more comfortable skin.

Here is the full method, the ingredients that help, and how to adjust it for Indian weather.

Why skin dries out at night

Skin loses water all the time, and it can lose more at night, especially in a cool, dry AC room or under a fan. Hard water, harsh face washes and long hot showers make this worse. If your face feels tight when you wake up, your evening routine is not holding enough moisture in.

Good overnight moisture works in two layers: something that pulls water into the skin, and something that sits on top to slow it from escaping.

How to moisturise skin overnight: step by step

  1. Cleanse gently. Use a mild face wash and lukewarm, not hot, water.
  2. Leave skin slightly damp. Pat with a towel, but do not dry completely.
  3. Add a hydrating layer (optional). A few drops of a hyaluronic acid or glycerin-based serum, pressed in.
  4. Apply night cream within a minute. A pea-sized amount for the face, a little more for the neck.
  5. Seal dry patches. A thin extra layer on dry cheeks, around the nose or on the lips.
  6. Sleep smart. A clean pillowcase, and avoid sleeping with a fan or AC blowing straight onto your face.

How to use night cream correctly

Most overnight dryness comes down to small mistakes with how night cream is used:

  • Too little: a tiny dab spread thin leaves gaps. A pea-sized amount is a good start.
  • On bone-dry skin: applying to damp skin helps lock in more water.
  • Too late: waiting 20 minutes after washing lets skin dry and tighten.
  • Rubbing hard: dot it on, then smooth upward and outward.

Glycerin moisturiser for dry skin

Glycerin is one of the most common and useful moisturising ingredients. It is a humectant, which means it draws water into the top layer of skin. It is gentle, inexpensive and found in many creams and serums.

A glycerin moisturiser for dry skin works best when it is followed by something richer, such as a cream with plant oils, to hold that water in. On its own, in a very dry room, a humectant can feel less effective.

Squalane moisturiser for dehydrated skin

Squalane is a light, non-greasy oil. It is an emollient: it softens skin and helps reduce water loss. A squalane moisturiser for dehydrated skin is popular because it feels silky rather than heavy, which suits humid Indian summers.

Adjusting for Indian weather

  • Winter: use a little more cream and seal dry patches.
  • AC all night: add a hydrating serum under your cream, or keep a bowl of water in the room.
  • Summer and monsoon: a thinner layer is enough; skip heavy oils if you are acne-prone.
  • Hard water: moisturise straight after washing.

If kojic acid is in your night cream, wear sunscreen the next morning. If dryness comes with cracking, bleeding, an itchy rash or eczema, see a dermatologist.
