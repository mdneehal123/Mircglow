The best monsoon skincare routine is a light one: cleanse well twice a day, use a gel or thin cream, keep sunscreen on even when it is cloudy, and dry your skin properly after you get wet. Rain and humidity make skin sweatier and stickier, so this is the season to do less, not more.

From Mumbai to Kolkata to Kerala, the monsoon brings the same problems: shine by noon, clogged pores, the odd breakout, and makeup that slides off. Here is a routine built for those months.

What the monsoon does to your skin

  • High humidity. Sweat and oil sit on the skin longer, so pores can look larger and feel clogged.
  • Getting wet and staying damp. Rainwater, damp collars and wet hair against the face can leave skin irritated.
  • Switching between rain and AC. Going from sticky streets into a cold office can leave skin oily on top and tight underneath.
  • Less sun, but not no sun. Clouds block some light, but UV still reaches the skin on grey days.

Your monsoon skincare routine: morning

  1. Gentle face wash. A mild gel cleanser clears the oil from the night. Avoid harsh, squeaky-clean washes; they can make skin produce more oil.
  2. Toner (optional). An alcohol-free toner feels fresh in humid weather. Skip it if your skin is already comfortable.
  3. Light moisturiser. A gel or a very thin layer of cream. Even oily skin needs some hydration, especially if you sit in AC all day.
  4. Sunscreen. Pick a gel or fluid texture with SPF 30 or more. This matters even more if your evening routine includes kojic acid or vitamin C.

Your monsoon routine: night

  1. Cleanse thoroughly. If you wore sunscreen or makeup, use a cleansing oil or micellar water first, then your face wash. This is the step that keeps pores clear in humid weather.
  2. Serum (optional). Niacinamide is a good monsoon choice because it helps skin look less shiny and pores look smaller.
  3. A thin layer of night cream. Use about half a pea for the whole face and press it in. Put a little more on dry areas and less on the T-zone. Our Avocado Korean Night Cream works well in a thin layer like this.

Monsoon tips for oily and acne-prone skin

  • Keep blotting paper or a clean tissue in your bag instead of washing your face again and again.
  • Do not touch or pick at bumps. It makes marks more likely.
  • Wash your face after you get caught in the rain, then moisturise lightly.
  • Change pillowcases and face towels more often. Damp fabric does not dry fully in this season.
  • Keep phone screens clean. They touch your cheek many times a day.

If your acne is painful, deep or cystic, see a dermatologist. A routine can help skin feel cleaner, but it is not a substitute for medical care.

Dry or sensitive skin in the rains

Not everyone gets oily in the monsoon. If you work in strong AC all day, your skin may still feel tight. Keep a creamier moisturiser for night, and use your richer cream only on the dry patches. If your skin is sensitive, avoid strong fragrances and introduce one new product at a time.

Small habits that make a big difference

  • Dry your face and neck properly after rain. Pat, do not rub.
  • Keep your hair off your face if it is wet.
  • Use a separate, clean towel for your face.
  • Store creams in a cool, dry place and close the lids tightly, so moisture does not get in.
  • Use a clean spatula or clean, dry fingers when you scoop cream from a jar.
  • Drink water. It is easy to forget when the weather is cool.

Common monsoon skincare mistakes

  1. Skipping sunscreen on grey days. UV still gets through clouds.
  2. Skipping moisturiser because skin looks oily. Dehydrated skin can look shinier, not less shiny.
  3. Over-scrubbing. Harsh scrubs irritate skin. A mild exfoliant once a week is enough.
  4. Heavy layers. Five creams on top of each other will feel sticky in this weather. Keep it to three or four steps.
  5. Trying many new products at once. If something irritates you, you will not know which one it was.

After the monsoon: switching your routine in October

Once the rains stop, most of India gets a few weeks of warm, sticky days and then cooler, drier nights, especially in the north. Skin that was greasy in August can start to feel tight by November. You don't need a new routine, just a few small changes:

  1. Keep the gentle cleanser, but drop any oil-control face wash you were using twice a day.
  2. Move from gel to cream at night. Start with a pea-sized amount and add a little more on the cheeks if they feel tight in the morning.
  3. Don't stop sunscreen. October sun is still strong in most cities.
  4. Watch for dry patches around the nose and on the cheekbones. Our guide to dry patches on face covers what to do.

Short answers

Do I need sunscreen in the monsoon? Yes. UV passes through clouds, so use SPF 30 or more every morning, ideally in a gel texture.

Should I stop using moisturiser in the rainy season? No. Switch to a lighter gel or a thin layer of cream instead.

How often should I wash my face in the monsoon? Twice a day, plus a quick rinse after getting wet in the rain. More than that can dry skin out.

Can I use night cream in the monsoon? Yes. Use about half a pea, pressed into the skin, with less on the oily T-zone.

Why do I break out more in the rainy season? Sweat, oil and damp fabrics can clog pores. Cleansing well and keeping towels and pillowcases fresh usually helps.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
