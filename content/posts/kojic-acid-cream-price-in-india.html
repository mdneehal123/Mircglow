Kojic acid in cosmetic creams is usually used at around 1–2%. A higher price does not mean more kojic acid, and stronger is not better: it can make skin dry or irritated if overused. Our guide to kojic acid side effects explains what to watch for.

How to work out price per gram and per month

  1. Price per gram: divide the price by the grams. ₹649 ÷ 30 g is about ₹21.6 per gram.
  2. Grams per month: a pea-sized amount for face and neck is roughly 0.3–0.5 g a night, so about 10–15 g a month.
  3. Monthly cost: multiply the two. At ₹21.6 per gram, that is about ₹215–₹325 a month.

Small jars often look cheaper but cost more per gram. They are still useful for a first try or for travel.

Why the extra ingredients matter more than the price

Kojic acid is there to help dark spots and uneven patches look less noticeable over time. On its own it can feel a little drying. That is why the base of the cream matters:

  • Niacinamide (vitamin B3) supports the barrier and helps tone look more even. See niacinamide and kojic acid together.
  • Aloe vera helps skin feel soothed and refreshed.
  • Avocado adds richness so skin feels soft rather than tight. Read avocado and kojic acid explained.

A slightly more expensive cream that you can use comfortably every night is usually better value than a cheap one that stings and ends up in a drawer.

Before you buy a kojic acid cream online

  • Check the full ingredient list and that kojic acid is named.
  • Look for batch number, manufacturing date and expiry.
  • Be wary of claims to change skin colour, or results in a few days. A more even look usually needs 8–12 weeks.
  • Check delivery charges, payment options and the return policy.

Use it well so the money is not wasted

  • Patch test behind the ear or on the jawline for two to three nights.
  • Start every other night if your skin is sensitive, then build up.
  • Use a pea-sized amount at night on clean, dry skin.
  • Wear sunscreen every morning. Without it, dark spots tend to come back and the cream cannot keep up.
  • Follow the routine in our kojic acid cream for dark spots guide.

Avoid kojic acid on broken or freshly waxed skin. If you are pregnant or have melasma, speak to a dermatologist first.
