A good skincare routine for combination skin uses the same simple steps on your whole face but different amounts: a gentle gel cleanser, a light moisturiser that is a bit generous on the cheeks and sparing on the T-zone, and sunscreen in the morning; then cleanse, one active if needed, and a medium-weight cream at night. You don't need two full routines. You need one routine and a little zone thinking.

Combination skin is very common in India, where humidity makes the forehead and nose shine while AC and hard water leave the cheeks tight. Here is a full morning and night plan, how to choose a face cream for combination skin, and how to add brightening care without upsetting either zone.

Do you have combination skin?

Wash your face, pat dry and leave it bare for an hour. You probably have combination skin if:

  • Your forehead, nose and chin look shiny or feel slightly oily.
  • Your cheeks feel normal, tight or a little dry.
  • Pores look larger on the nose than on the cheeks.
  • Your skin feels oilier in summer and drier in winter.

If you are unsure, try the skin quiz.

Morning skincare routine for combination skin

  1. Cleanse. A gentle gel or low-foam face wash. Avoid strong "oil control" washes that leave cheeks rough.
  2. Hydrating toner (optional). One layer patted all over. Plain hydrating toners suit both zones.
  3. Moisturise by zone. A pea-sized amount of light moisturiser: press most of it into the cheeks, then spread what is left over the T-zone.
  4. Sunscreen. SPF 30 or higher over the whole face. A gel-cream or fluid texture usually suits both zones.

By afternoon, blot the T-zone with blotting paper rather than washing again.

Night routine for combination skin

  1. Cleanse well. Micellar water or a cleansing balm first if you wore sunscreen, then your gel face wash.
  2. One active, a few nights a week. Niacinamide is a friendly choice because it suits both oily and dry areas. If you use salicylic acid, put it on the T-zone only.
  3. Night cream by zone. More on the cheeks, only the leftover on the T-zone. Our night cream for combination skin guide explains this zone method step by step.

A face cream for combination skin should not feel greasy on the T-zone 15 minutes after applying. If it does, use less there or switch that area to a gel.

Brightening cream for combination skin

Many people with combination skin also notice tanning, a dull look or old acne marks, often on the cheeks and around the mouth. A brightening cream for combination skin can help skin look brighter and more even over time if you choose and use it well:

  • Use it at night. Ingredients like kojic acid, alpha arbutin, vitamin C and glutathione are best used in the evening, with sunscreen every morning.
  • Thin layer, zone method. Brightening creams still go more on the cheeks and less on an oily nose.
  • Start slowly. 3–4 nights a week for the first two weeks.
  • Be patient. Skin renews roughly every 4–6 weeks, so expect 8–12 weeks of steady use for a more even look.

A brightening cream helps skin look brighter and more even. It does not change your natural skin colour, and it can't do much without daily sunscreen. Read glutathione cream benefits for what this kind of cream can realistically do.

Common mistakes

  • Using a strong oily-skin face wash on the whole face.
  • Skipping moisturiser because the nose is shiny.
  • Putting the same thick layer everywhere.
  • Scrubbing the T-zone daily.
  • Starting two brightening actives in the same week.

See a dermatologist if you have painful or cystic acne, redness that does not settle, or dark patches that keep spreading (which can be melasma). Ask before starting actives if you are pregnant.

Short answers

Do I need two routines for combination skin? No. One routine with more moisturiser on the cheeks and less on the T-zone works for most people.

Which face cream suits combination skin? A light cream or gel-cream with humectants and niacinamide, used in different amounts on each zone.

Can I use a brightening cream on combination skin? Yes, at night in a thin layer, with sunscreen every morning.

Is niacinamide good for combination skin? Yes. It suits oily and dry areas and helps skin look more balanced.

How long does a new routine take to show? About 4–6 weeks for comfort and texture, and 8–12 weeks for tone to look more even.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
