A good skincare routine for oily skin in India has just four steps in the morning (gentle cleanser, light moisturiser, sunscreen, optional toner) and three at night (cleanse, one active if you use it, thin moisturiser or night cream). The goal is not to dry the oil out. It is to keep skin clean, comfortable and protected so shine and clogged pores are easier to manage.

Heat, humidity, pollution and long hours outdoors all make oily skin feel oilier here. This guide gives a year-round plan that works for women and men, plus what to expect to spend.

What oily skin really needs

Oily skin makes more sebum, often on the forehead, nose and chin. Pores look larger and makeup slides by afternoon. But oily skin is often short on water too, especially if you use harsh face washes or sit in AC. When it feels stripped, it can look shinier and more uneven.

  • Gentle cleansing twice a day, not five times.
  • Light hydration that sinks in quickly.
  • Daily sunscreen in a gel or fluid texture.
  • One active at a time, such as niacinamide or salicylic acid.

Morning skincare routine for oily skin in India

  1. Cleanse. A mild gel or foam face wash. Lukewarm water, 30 seconds, pat dry.
  2. Toner (optional). A plain hydrating or niacinamide toner if you like the feel. Skip astringents with lots of alcohol.
  3. Moisturise. A pea-sized amount of gel or gel-cream. If your cheeks feel normal and only your T-zone is oily, you may have combination skin; see our night cream for combination skin guide for the zone method.
  4. Sunscreen. SPF 30 or higher, two finger-lengths for face and neck. Reapply every 2–3 hours outdoors. A matte or gel sunscreen helps on very oily skin.

Carry blotting paper instead of washing again at lunch. Pressing (not rubbing) takes shine off without disturbing sunscreen.

Night routine for oily skin

  1. Cleanse well. If you wore sunscreen or makeup, use micellar water or a cleansing balm first, then your face wash.
  2. One active, a few nights a week. Salicylic acid helps keep pores clearer; niacinamide helps skin look more balanced. Start one, not both at once.
  3. Thin layer of moisturiser or night cream. Rice-grain to pea-sized amount. Our night cream for oily skin guide covers texture and how much to use.

Once or twice a week, a clay mask on the T-zone can make skin feel fresher. Avoid daily scrubs; they tend to make oily skin look redder, not clearer.

Skincare routine for oily and uneven skin

Oily skin often comes with tanning, old acne marks and a slightly patchy tone. A skincare routine for oily and uneven skin keeps the same base, then adds:

  • Sunscreen every single morning, because sun makes marks look darker for longer.
  • Niacinamide, which helps skin look more even and pores look smaller.
  • Kojic acid or alpha arbutin in a night product for a more even-looking tone. Kojic acid is usually used around 1–2% in creams.
  • Patience. Skin renews roughly every 4–6 weeks, so expect 8–12 weeks of steady use before tone looks more even.

Mistakes that make oily skin look oilier

  • Washing too often or with very hot water.
  • Skipping moisturiser or sunscreen.
  • Using heavy oils like coconut oil on the whole face.
  • Scrubbing every day.
  • Trying three new products in one week.

See a dermatologist if you have painful or cystic acne, sudden breakouts in adulthood, or dark patches that keep spreading. Ask before starting acids if you are pregnant.

Short answers

How many times should I wash oily skin? Twice a day is enough. Splash with plain water or use blotting paper in between.

Does oily skin need moisturiser in Indian summer? Yes, a thin layer of gel moisturiser keeps skin comfortable without adding shine.

What is the best routine for men with oily skin? Gel face wash, light moisturiser and sunscreen in the morning, and face wash plus a thin moisturiser at night.

Can oily skin use night cream? Yes, a rice-grain to pea-sized amount of a light night cream is fine.

How long before oily skin looks better? Give a steady routine 4–6 weeks, and 8–12 weeks for tone to look more even.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
