A good winter skincare routine in India is simple: a gentle cleanser, a richer moisturiser on slightly damp skin, sunscreen every morning and a thicker night cream before bed. Winter here is not the same everywhere, so the trick is to match how much you add to how dry the air in your city really gets.

In Delhi or Lucknow, skin can feel tight by lunchtime. In Chennai or Kochi, winter is just a milder version of the rest of the year. This guide shows you what to change, step by step, and what to leave alone.

Why skin feels different in an Indian winter

  • Dry air. In north and central India, humidity drops and water leaves the skin faster. Cheeks and the area around the nose often feel tight first.
  • Hot showers. A long, hot bath feels lovely on a cold morning, but it strips oil from the skin and leaves it rough.
  • Heaters and blowers. Room heaters dry the air indoors, the same way AC does in summer.
  • Smog. Winter pollution in many northern cities settles on the face, so the evening cleanse matters more.
  • The sun is still there. It feels softer, but UV is present all year. Winter is not a holiday from sunscreen.

Your winter skincare routine in India: morning

  1. Cleanse gently, or just rinse. If your skin is dry, lukewarm water is often enough in the morning. Oily skin can use a mild, non-foaming face wash.
  2. Hydrating toner or mist (optional). Pat it in while the skin is still a little damp.
  3. Moisturiser. Use a slightly richer cream than you use in summer. A small amount of the Avocado Glow Cream works well as a daytime layer under sunscreen.
  4. Sunscreen. Broad-spectrum, SPF 30 or more. Do this every day, especially if you use anything with kojic acid or vitamin C.
  5. Lip balm. Lips have very little oil of their own and crack first in winter.

Your winter routine: night

  1. Cleanse. If you wore sunscreen or makeup, use an oil cleanser or balm first, then a gentle face wash.
  2. Toner or serum (optional). Hyaluronic acid or niacinamide are both easy choices for winter.
  3. Night cream. This is the step that does the most in winter. Use a full pea-sized amount for the face and a little more for the neck. Our Avocado Night Cream is a richer option that helps dry skin feel soft and moisturised by morning.
  4. Extra care for very dry patches. A thin layer of plain moisturiser or petroleum jelly over flaky areas, like the sides of the nose, can help.

Adjusting for your skin type

Dry skin

Winter is hardest on you. Use a cream cleanser, moisturise within a minute of washing, and do not skip the night cream. Read more in our guide to avocado cream for dry skin.

Oily or combination skin

You still need moisture in winter. Oily skin that is short on water can actually feel greasier. Keep a light gel by day and use a thin layer of night cream, more on the cheeks and less on the T-zone. Our night cream for oily skin guide explains how.

Sensitive skin

Cut back to the basics: mild cleanser, plain moisturiser, sunscreen. Introduce one new product at a time and patch test it first on the inner arm or behind the ear for a day or two.

Habits that help more than another product

  • Bathe in lukewarm water, not hot, and keep it short.
  • Pat your face dry with a soft towel. Do not rub.
  • Apply moisturiser on slightly damp skin to hold the water in.
  • Drink water through the day, even when you do not feel thirsty.
  • If you use a room heater, keep a bowl of water in the room or step out for fresh air.
  • Change your pillowcase every week.

Common winter mistakes

  1. Skipping sunscreen because it is cold or foggy. UV still reaches the skin, and dark spots can look more noticeable over time.
  2. Scrubbing away flakes. Harsh scrubs make dry skin rougher. A gentle exfoliant once a week is enough.
  3. Using too many actives. Winter skin is more easily irritated. If you use a kojic acid cream, start it every other night. Our kojic acid cream guide explains how to ease in.
  4. Applying thick cream on dry skin. Cream works better on skin that is slightly damp.
  5. Forgetting the neck and hands. They dry out just as quickly as the face.

When to see a dermatologist

Dryness that cracks or bleeds, red itchy patches that do not settle, or flaking that spreads may be a skin condition such as eczema, not just winter dryness. See a dermatologist if this happens, and also before starting new actives if you are pregnant.

Short answers

Do I need sunscreen in winter in India? Yes. UV is present all year, so apply SPF 30 or more every morning.

Should I use a heavier night cream in winter? Usually, yes. Dry and normal skin do well with a richer cream at night, while oily skin can stay with a thin layer.

Can I use face wash twice a day in winter? At night, yes. In the morning, dry skin often needs only lukewarm water.

Why does my skin feel oily and dry at the same time? It is often short on water, not oil. A light moisturiser and gentler cleansing usually help it feel balanced.

Is coconut oil enough for winter skin? It can help the body, but on the face it may feel heavy and some people get breakouts. A face cream is a safer daily choice.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
