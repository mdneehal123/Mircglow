Spa and resort use: what works in Goa's climate

Goa is warm and humid most of the year, with a long, heavy monsoon from around June to September. Many spa clients are tourists who have spent the day in strong sun and salt water, then sleep in air-conditioned rooms. That mix tells you how to stock:

  • For after-sun facials and sensitive, sun-warmed skin, use the plain avocado glow cream. It has no kojic acid or glutathione, so it is the gentler pick for skin that already feels hot or tight.
  • For regular evening facials on humid days, a thin layer of avocado Korean night cream is usually enough.
  • Keep a smaller tub of the richer avocado night cream for guests with dry skin from AC and sun.
  • Remind guests that creams with kojic acid or glutathione should be followed by sunscreen the next morning, which matters a lot on a beach holiday.

Storage in a spa or resort

  • Keep the main tub sealed in a dry store room, not in the steam room, a massage room or near showers.
  • Decant a day's supply into a small jar for each therapist, and use clean, dry spatulas.
  • Water getting into the tub is the most common reason bulk cream spoils early, so never dip wet fingers or wet tools.
  • Through the monsoon, order smaller quantities more often.
