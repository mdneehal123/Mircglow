A good night cream for your 40s is usually richer than what you used at 30, with ingredients that hold in moisture (hyaluronic acid, plant oils like avocado) and ones that help skin look more even and smoother (niacinamide). Put it on your neck as well as your face, and wear sunscreen every morning. That combination does more than any single expensive jar.

Below we explain what tends to change in the 40s, which ingredients make sense, and how to put together a routine that's easy to keep up.

What changes in your 40s

Not everyone's skin changes at the same pace, but most women in India notice some of the same things around this time. Skin feels drier, especially after a bath or in a Delhi or Lucknow winter. Fine lines around the eyes and mouth look more obvious on days when skin is dehydrated. Old sun exposure starts to show as patchy, uneven-looking tone on the cheeks and forehead. And skin takes longer to bounce back from a late night, a wedding weekend or a long train ride.

Part of this is natural ageing. Part of it is hormonal, as many women move towards perimenopause in their 40s, which often makes skin feel drier. A lot of it is simply years of sun. None of this is a problem to "fix". It's a reason to give skin a bit more moisture and a lot more sun protection than before.

If your skin changes suddenly, itches a lot, or you see patches that are spreading, see a dermatologist rather than switching creams.

Why a richer cream usually feels better now

In your 20s a light gel-cream may have been plenty. In your 40s, the same jar can leave skin feeling tight by midnight. A creamier texture with more plant oils sits on the skin a little longer and keeps water from escaping overnight. In the morning, skin tends to feel softer and look less tired.

That said, weather still matters. If you're in Chennai or Mumbai and your skin is still on the oily side, you don't need a heavy balm. Use a medium cream in summer and save the richer one for winter or AC-heavy months. The goal is skin that feels comfortable, not coated.

Don't forget the neck

The neck has thinner skin, fewer oil glands and gets just as much sun as your face, yet most of us stop the cream at the jawline. In your 40s, lines and uneven-looking tone often show on the neck first.

Take a little extra cream after doing your face and smooth it upwards from the collarbone to the jaw. Light pressure, no pulling. And yes, sunscreen goes on the neck too. Our night cream on neck guide covers the how-to.

A night and morning routine for women over 40

Night

  1. Cleanse gently. If you wore makeup or sunscreen, start with a cleansing balm or micellar water.
  2. Pat dry, leaving skin slightly damp.
  3. Optional: a hydrating serum (hyaluronic acid) or niacinamide.
  4. Night cream on face and neck. A pea-sized amount for the face, a little more for the neck.
  5. On very dry nights, a second thin layer on cheeks and around the mouth.

Morning

  1. Rinse or a mild cleanse.
  2. A light moisturiser if skin feels dry.
  3. Sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, on face, neck and the backs of your hands. Reapply if you're out in the sun.

Skin renews roughly every 4 to 6 weeks, so judge a new cream after 8 to 12 weeks of steady use, not after a few days.

Short answers

What is the best night cream for women over 40? One with a richer texture plus niacinamide and hyaluronic acid or plant oils, used every night with daily sunscreen.

Should I start retinol in my 40s? You can, slowly, two or three nights a week at first. It's not essential, and it isn't for everyone.

Can a night cream get rid of wrinkles? No. Good hydration can soften the look of fine lines, but a cream won't take wrinkles away.

Should I put night cream on my neck? Yes. The neck shows dryness and sun early, so use the same cream there.

How long before I see a difference? Skin feels softer quickly. A more even look usually takes 8 to 12 weeks with sunscreen.

This is general skincare information, not medical advice. Patch test any new product, and see a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
